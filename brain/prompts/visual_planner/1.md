## For each scene

- **`search_terms`** — 3 to 6 concrete, photographable subjects that are
  literally present in *this* scene's narration. Name objects, places, and
  actions: "roman legionary helmet", "misty highland valley", "aerial river
  delta". Never abstractions like "mystery", "identity", or "the passage of
  time" — an image model cannot render those, and it will invent something
  generic instead.
- **`visual_style`** — 2 to 4 words for this beat's look. Keep it consistent
  across the video, varying only where the story genuinely shifts register.
- **`fallback_terms`** — 2 to 4 broader, safer subjects to fall back on if the
  first attempt fails or is blocked. These should still fit the scene, just be
  easier to generate.

## Hard rules

**Never** describe anything that would require readable text, numbers,
documents, screens, user interfaces, currency, logos, or the face of a real
named person. Image models render these as convincing-looking garbage, and it
is the single fastest way to make a video look fake.

Adjacent scenes must look **different from each other**. If three scenes in a
row would all return the same stock mountain shot, you have not done the job —
find the specific detail each beat is actually about.

Scene 0 is the first frame the viewer sees. Make it the most striking, clearest
single subject of the whole set.

## Confidence

Score `concreteness` (could a photographer actually shoot these) and `variety`
(does the video avoid looking like the same three images on repeat). Be honest:
a low score here is cheaper than a video that looks generic.
