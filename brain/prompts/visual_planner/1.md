## Two visual modes

### Mode 1: AI Image (default for most scenes)
Generate a cinematic shot description for an AI image model.

- **`search_terms`** — 4 to 6 concrete visual elements as a cinematic shot:
  - Start with the subject: "close-up of weathered hands gripping a ship's helm"
  - Add environment: "storm clouds over churning Atlantic ocean"
  - Camera/lighting cue: "low-angle dramatic backlight, spray particles"
  - Time/mood: "golden hour", "overcast dawn", "torch-lit darkness"

### Mode 2: Motion Graphics Template (for data, lists, stats, reveals)
Use a template when the narration presents **structured information** that
benefits from animated text/graphics:

- **`template_category`** — one of:
  - `"timeline"` — chronological events (year + title pairs)
  - `"ranking"` — ordered lists with values (top 5, largest, etc.)
  - `"chart"` — bar/line/pie charts with numeric data
  - `"counter"` — a single dramatic number reveal (distance, population, etc.)
  - `"data"` — stats cards, progress bars, gauges
  - `"list"` — bullet points, feature lists, key facts
  - `"text"` — dramatic text reveal for a key phrase or quote
  - `"roller"` — countdown, split-flap reveal of a word/number
  - `"cinematic"` — title cards, dramatic mood transitions
  - `"reveal"` — general dramatic information reveal

- **`template_data`** — the content for the template. Match the structure:
  - timeline: `{"events": [{"year": "1947", "title": "Independence"}, ...]}`
  - ranking: `{"items": [{"name": "China", "value": "1.4B"}, ...], "title": "Largest Populations"}`
  - counter: `{"from": 0, "to": 4300, "unit": "km", "label": "TOTAL LENGTH"}`
  - chart: `{"items": [{"label": "Pakistan", "value": 80}, ...], "title": "Strategic Importance"}`
  - list: `{"items": ["Fact one", "Fact two", ...], "title": "Key Reasons"}`
  - text: `{"text": "The key phrase to reveal"}`

### Mode 3: Both (template overlaid on image)
When both `search_terms` AND `template_category` are set, the image renders as a
dimmed background behind the animated template. Use this for data scenes that
still benefit from a relevant atmospheric image behind them.

## Decision rules

**Use a TEMPLATE when the narration:**
- Lists multiple items, facts, or comparisons
- States a specific number/statistic that deserves emphasis
- Walks through a chronological sequence
- Ranks or compares things
- Reveals a key quote or phrase dramatically

**Use an IMAGE when the narration:**
- Describes a place, person, object, or event visually
- Sets an atmosphere or mood
- Shows cause-and-effect or spatial relationships
- Doesn't contain structured/enumerable data

**Use BOTH when:**
- You have data to show but the scene also benefits from a visual anchor
- A ranking/stat looks better with an atmospheric background

## Balance

Aim for **70-80% image scenes, 15-25% template scenes, 5-10% both**. Templates
are slow to render — a 50-scene video with all templates takes over 30 minutes.
Use templates ONLY for scenes where structured data (numbers, lists, timelines)
is the main point. Most scenes should be images.

**Hard limit**: no more than 10 template scenes per video, regardless of total
scene count. If you have more than 10 data-heavy scenes, pick the 10 most
impactful and use images for the rest.

## Image rules (when using search_terms)

1. Every scene must be a **distinct composition** — vary distance, angle, lighting
2. Scene 0 is the **hook frame** — most dramatic, highest contrast
3. **Avoid**: readable text, numbers, documents, UIs, faces of real named people
4. **Prefer**: dramatic lighting, strong foreground/background, natural textures
5. **Content policy**: Never depict graphic violence, weapons being used on people,
   blood, gore, or explicit destruction of human life. For war/conflict scenes,
   frame through: aftermath (ruins, empty battlefield at dawn), symbolism (broken
   sword on stone, scattered shields), wide aerial views, or maps/strategy.
   The image model WILL reject violent prompts and the scene degrades to a
   placeholder — which looks worse than a well-chosen non-violent alternative.

## Confidence

Score `concreteness` (photographable? data well-structured?) and `variety`
(visual diversity across the video? good image-to-template rhythm?).
