## For each scene

- **`search_terms`** — 4 to 6 concrete visual elements written as a **cinematic
  shot description**. Think like a cinematographer, not a stock photographer:
  - Start with the subject: "close-up of weathered hands gripping a ship's helm"
  - Add environment context: "storm clouds over churning Atlantic ocean"
  - Include a camera/lighting cue: "low-angle dramatic backlight, spray particles"
  - Specify time/mood: "golden hour", "overcast dawn", "torch-lit darkness"

  **DO NOT** write generic concepts like "geography", "history", or "politics".
  **DO** write what the camera literally frames: "aerial view of the Strait of
  Hormuz at sunset, oil tankers in shipping lanes, desert coastline both sides".

- **`visual_style`** — A consistent cinematic direction for this scene. Examples:
  "cinematic documentary aerial", "dramatic chiaroscuro baroque painting",
  "hyperrealistic macro photography", "moody desaturated war journalism".
  Keep it consistent across the whole video unless the story genuinely shifts
  register (e.g. from historical to modern).

- **`fallback_terms`** — 3 to 4 broader but still visual subjects. These must
  still be frameable as a photograph. Instead of "Chilean economy", write
  "copper mine terraces aerial view, red desert landscape".

## Cinematography rules

1. **Every scene must be a distinct composition.** No two adjacent scenes should
   look like the same stock photo. Vary: subject distance (close-up vs aerial),
   angle (low vs eye-level vs birds-eye), lighting direction, and color palette.

2. **Scene 0 is the thumbnail/hook frame.** Make it the most dramatic, highest-
   contrast, clearest single-subject image in the set. One focal point, punchy
   color, instant readability at mobile scale.

3. **The payoff scene (second to last) should be the emotional peak** — the most
   visually striking composition, often a reveal or contrast.

4. **Avoid**: readable text, numbers, documents, screens, UIs, currency, logos,
   recognizable faces of real named people. These render as obvious AI artifacts.

5. **Prefer**: dramatic lighting, strong foreground/background separation, one
   clear focal subject per frame, natural textures (stone, water, metal, fabric),
   and real-world scale references that help the viewer feel the scene.

## Confidence

Score `concreteness` (could a photographer actually shoot these exact frames?)
and `variety` (does a viewer scrolling through 25 thumbnails see 25 different
images, or the same three mountain/map shots on repeat?). Be honest: a low
score here is cheaper than a video that looks generic.
