## The rule that matters

**Every scene the review marked `weak` must come back visibly different.** A release gate compares your output against the plan above, scene by scene, and rejects a revision where a flagged scene is unchanged. Re-emitting a flagged scene as-is fails the whole revision, so if you disagree with a finding, still change the scene -- make the smallest change that answers the note.

**Every scene the review did NOT mark `weak` must come back unchanged.** Copy those scenes across exactly. Rewriting scenes nobody complained about is how a revision pass makes an episode worse: it churns entity spelling, which breaks the renderer's cross-scene identity, and it trades a working scene for an unreviewed one.

## Acting on each failure mode

- `generic-visual`: change what is drawn, not the words on it. Pick the primitive that depicts this specific claim, and author the `model_relations` that make its structure this episode's rather than the fallback's. Renaming labels on the same generic graphic is not a fix.
- `claim-mismatch`: correct the direction or the state. If the note says a relation runs backwards, swap `from_element` and `to_element`. If the scene is still entertaining a guess, `visual_state` is `hypothesis`, not `mechanism`.
- `static`: choose an operation that progresses across the whole spoken turn, matching setup to the first clause and consequence to the last. If the primitive genuinely has no moving part for this line, change the primitive.
- `repeats-neighbour`: change this scene's primitive or operation so the two read as different shots. Do not change the neighbour -- it was not flagged.
- `unauthored-structure`: author the `model_relations` the line actually claims. Use `blocks` where the line says one thing prevents another, `feeds` for continuous supply, `becomes` for one thing turning into another, `contains` for enclosure, `causes` otherwise. Draw only the connections the line makes; three deliberate edges beat six arbitrary ones.
- `overfull-label`: cut the field to a phrase. `state_before`/`state_after` render in a fixed 2-line box and must sit well under 32 characters; `key_text` under 48. "sealed cells", not "the cells look sealed before the knife touches them".

Also act on `episode_note` where it names something you can fix inside a scene -- a repeated primitive across a stretch, a payoff that does not resolve the opening model.

## Constraints you must not break

- Scene 0 stays a `character-hook` bookend with `character_cut_in: "both"`; the final content scene stays a `recap` with `visual_operation: "payoff"` and `visual_state: "payoff"`.
- Keep entity spelling stable across scenes. The renderer keys an entity's shape and colour off its wording; respelling an entity between scenes makes the same thing appear as two unrelated marks.
- `model_relations` indices are zero-based positions in that same scene's `model_elements`. An index past the end of the array is dropped and its connection disappears.
- Subject primitives (`particles`, `wave`, `rays`, `horizon`, `spectrum`, `path`, `shells`) take `model_relations: []`.
- `numeric_value` is mandatory and a bare number whenever `visual_primitive` is `quantity` or `visual_operation` is `counter`, and omitted otherwise. One scene breaking this rejects the whole plan.
- Character cut-ins stay at or under 35% of scenes.
