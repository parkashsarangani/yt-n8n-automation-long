## How to score

Read the narration straight through as if you were hearing it, with no visuals — that is the honest test of this format, since the images support the story but the words carry it. Score each dimension in [0, 1]:

- **hook**: Does the first 1-2 scenes create a real question a viewer wants answered in the next few seconds? A topic announcement or a slow wind-up scores low even if everything after it is good.
- **suspense**: Does tension escalate through the middle, or does it go flat, repeat itself, or resolve too early? A story that peaks in act 2 and coasts scores low here even if the ending is fine.
- **watchability**: Read purely as spoken narration, scene to scene, does it hold attention? Penalize sentences that only work on the page, dead scenes that don't move the story, and any scene where a viewer would plausibly tap away.
- **entertainment**: Wit, surprise, specificity, a real point of view. Generic, safe, or over-explained narration scores low even if it is technically well-organized.
- **payoff**: Does the ending land? It must earn the setup and hit harder than the opening — a flat summary, a stated moral, or a payoff that just restates the premise scores low.
- **youtube_fit**: Is this premise titleable, thumbnailable, shareable? Would a real viewer send this to someone? Score low if the story is only interesting in the abstract, has no single shareable image or moment, or needs prior context to land.

Do not score factual accuracy, completeness, or whether the story teaches something correctly. Those are explicitly not this gate's job — a story can be simplified, exaggerated, or told as parable and still score well here, and a story that is scrupulously accurate but flat scores poorly.

## Verdict

`pass` if the story is genuinely ready to produce as-is. `revise` if a real fix would make it meaningfully more watchable — reserve this for fixes actually worth another writing pass, not nitpicks.

## weakest_dimension and summary

Name the single dimension most likely to make a viewer click away, and in `summary`, say concretely where in the script that happens and why (cite the scene or moment) — not a general restatement of the score.

## Confidence

Score `evaluation_reliability`: low if the story's genre or tone made any dimension genuinely hard to judge (e.g. deliberately anticlimactic by design, or a format you're unsure how to weigh), high if you're confident the scores reflect how a real audience would react.
