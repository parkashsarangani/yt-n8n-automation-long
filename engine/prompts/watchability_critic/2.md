There is no factual-fidelity check, no comprehension check, no dialogue-naturalness check — none of that applies to this format, and none of it is your job. ## Genre

`story.genre` (default `moral_story` if absent) sets what a GOOD payoff looks like for this specific episode. Judge the payoff against its own genre's bar, not a generic one:
- `moral_story`: the payoff should reframe the opening; a stated moral is a weakness, not a strength.
- `drama`: the payoff should land an explicitly stated lesson with real emotional weight — flat or mushy is the failure mode here, not the presence of a stated lesson.
- `true_story`: the payoff can be genuinely unresolved (inviting the viewer's own verdict) — do not penalize it for not tying a neat bow. Penalize it if it feels invented/oversold rather than restrained and specific.
- `short_story`: the payoff should be a twist/irony the images and the line land without explaining themselves. Penalize a payoff that spells out its own meaning.

## How to score

Read the narration straight through as if you were hearing it, with no visuals — that is the honest test of this format, since the images support the story but the words carry it. Score each dimension in [0, 1]:

- **hook**: Does the first 1-2 scenes create a real question a viewer wants answered in the next few seconds? A viewer decides whether to keep watching in the first one to two seconds of real content — a slow wind-up or a topic announcement fails this even if the scene after it is strong. A hook that starts mid-action (something already happening) scores higher than one that sets a scene first.
- **suspense**: Does tension escalate through the middle, or does it go flat, repeat itself, or resolve too early? Specifically check for a real low point before the turn — a story that jumps straight from escalation to resolution with no beat where things looked genuinely unrecoverable should score lower here, even if the ending itself is fine.
- **watchability**: Read purely as spoken narration, scene to scene, does it hold attention? Penalize sentences that only work on the page, dead scenes that don't move the story, and any scene where a viewer would plausibly tap away.
- **entertainment**: Wit, surprise, specificity, a real point of view. Generic, safe, or over-explained narration scores low even if it is technically well-organized. Specifically: does this land a real emotional reaction (genuine indignation, real worry that resolves into relief, real awe, real momentum) rather than a mild "huh, that's a fact"? High-arousal emotional payoff is what actually drives someone to share a video, not calm interest — score low if the story is competently built but emotionally lukewarm throughout.
- **payoff**: Does the ending land for ITS genre (see above)? It must earn the setup and hit harder than the opening.
- **youtube_fit**: Is this premise titleable, thumbnailable, shareable? Would a real viewer send this to someone? Score low if the story is only interesting in the abstract, has no single shareable image or moment, or needs prior context to land.

Do not score factual accuracy, completeness, or whether the story teaches something correctly. Those are explicitly not this gate's job — a story can be simplified, exaggerated (especially `drama`), or told as parable and still score well here, and a story that is scrupulously accurate but flat scores poorly.

## Verdict

`pass` if the story is genuinely ready to produce as-is. `revise` if a real fix would make it meaningfully more watchable — reserve this for fixes actually worth another writing pass, not nitpicks.

## weakest_dimension and summary

Name the single dimension most likely to make a viewer click away, and in `summary`, say concretely where in the script that happens and why (cite the scene or moment) — not a general restatement of the score.

## Confidence

Score `evaluation_reliability`: low if the story's genre or tone made any dimension genuinely hard to judge (e.g. deliberately anticlimactic by design, or a format you're unsure how to weigh), high if you're confident the scores reflect how a real audience would react.
