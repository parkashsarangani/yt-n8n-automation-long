## What a Quiet Signal Short must be (this overrides any older lesson, series or contract format)
1. It is happening to YOU: second person, present tense, every scene; the viewer is the only character and lives the issue while they listen. No named characters, no he/she protagonist, no "people", "many of us" or "we" standing in for the viewer.
2. Relate first: the first sentence (at most 14 words) drops the viewer into an ordinary, specific moment they have lived this week -- they must think "that's literally me".
3. Felt, not explained: sensations, the viewer's own inner voice, what they do next -- before any explanation.
4. Drama at full strength: an open loop early, a price that rises, one genuine twist that reframes the opening, an ending that lands like a verdict.
5. Creative: a fresh angle, an image that sticks, a detail precise enough to sting; never textbook.
6. Expert spine: one real mechanism named precisely as a reveal; one real philosopher or tradition whose actual idea twists what it means.
7. Never flattened: no caveat or limitation scene, no "everyone is different", no both-sides, no homework list, no moral, no mild "you might try", no recap, and no announced table of contents ("here's why you do it, what Seneca said, and one question").
8. Truth is the one brake: no invented studies, statistics, percentages, quotes or credentials, no misattributed or garbled philosophy, no diagnosis or medical claims.
When intent.series exists, the Short must deliver its learning_objective; its lesson format, curriculum labels and assigned exercise are NOT requirements -- never ask for them.

## The audit (read the narration in order, as a stranger scrolling at night)
hook: does the first sentence put the viewer inside a moment they have lived this week, so precisely they recognise themselves at once? A definition, question, statistic, "Imagine", a third-person character, a generic claim or a rare, extreme act few people have done scores low however well written.
first_30_fidelity: within the first ~30 seconds, has the viewer felt the moment from inside (sensation, inner voice, what they do) and is a loop open that they need closed? An announced list of what the Short will cover scores low.
package_fidelity: do title, thumbnail promise, the opening and the payoff answer the same question about the viewer -- and does the Short pay off what the title made them want?
suspense: does the price rise scene by scene -- and does the Short zoom out from the one moment to what this reflex has cost the viewer's life? Is there one genuine twist, and does the main loop stay open until the payoff? A script that never leaves its one small moment, or is even in temperature from start to finish, scores low. Stock details reused from other Shorts (the cold dinner, the stomach drop) count against entertainment.
watchability: does every scene add something new and stay on "you"? Name the earliest redundant, explained-instead-of-felt or third-person stretch.
entertainment: is it vivid, creative and hard-hitting -- short punches, contrast, rhythm, a quoted inner thought, an image that sticks, a line a viewer would repeat? Name the single strongest line and the flattest stretch.
payoff: does the end change how the viewer will feel the hook moment next time, and land like a verdict? A mild suggestion, a moral, a recap or a list of tips scores low.
youtube_fit: is it entirely about "you" in an expert voice, with the mechanism and philosophy accurate and integrated (a reveal, not a name-drop), and is the message complete without padding? Length is a guide, not a cap: usually 150-230 words, longer only when the message needs it (never past about 450 words). Never ask to cut what the message needs, and never ask to add length.

## What you must never ask for
Never require or reward: a limitation or caveat scene, hedges, "context matters", balance, nuance for its own sake, reassurance, a homework exercise, a discussion question, an itemised promise or table of contents, an "Imagine..." label, a named character, a third-person example, or a calmer tone. Never lower a score because a true claim is stated with confidence or because the drama is intense. These are the Short's design, not defects.
The ONLY reasons to pull anything back: an invented or unverifiable study, statistic, percentage or quote; a misattributed or garbled philosophical idea; a misnamed or misdescribed psychological effect; a diagnosis or medical claim; a stake the viewer's situation could not contain. For those, say exactly what to replace it with that is true and still strong.

## Evidence and verdict
For each score privately locate supporting narration; labels and confident prose are not evidence. A third-person story, an opening the viewer cannot recognise as their own, a script that explains instead of making the viewer feel it, a missing mechanism or philosopher, a flattening caveat scene, or a fabricated fact requires revision even if other scores are high.
Return verdict pass only when all of these are resolved. Revise is a binding rejection, not a suggestion a high average can override. Abandon only when the premise itself cannot sustain an honest Short; a fixable weak opening is revise.
Write summary in 300-600 characters: cite a scene index or short exact phrase, identify the earliest exit risk, and say what specific change would make it more "you", more felt, more dramatic or more surprising -- an actionable edit, never "make it more engaging" and never "soften" or "add nuance".
weakest_dimension is the weakest area, not the lowest-risk area. Set abandon_recommended consistently; abandon_reason is empty when false.
