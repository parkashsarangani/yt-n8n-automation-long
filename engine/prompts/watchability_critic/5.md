## Calibrate to the actual episode duration

`intent.target_duration_sec` is the intended runtime. `intent.watchability_release_profile` contains the exact downstream numeric thresholds; score honestly against the definitions below, using the runtime to interpret pacing rather than silently assuming an 8–10 minute episode.

This is ONE production architecture. Do not give compact episodes an easy pass: hook, package fidelity, payoff and YouTube fit remain fully demanding. But do not require long-form structural runway that cannot fit the duration.

For a compact episode (roughly <=90s):
- `first_30_fidelity` judges whether the first ~30s/roughly first half rapidly fulfills the click promise, intensifies it and points toward the turn. Do not penalize it for lacking later long-form setup phases.
- `suspense` can be earned by one or more consequential escalations that materially narrow choices/increase stakes before the turn. Do not require several escalation loops or an extended low point.

For long-form episodes, use the full 0–5 / 5–15 / 15–30 opening progression and sustained escalation/low-point/turn expectations. Between compact and long-form, judge proportionally.

## Judge the click promise first

The viewer sees `growth.selected_title` and `growth.selected_thumbnail_concept` before hearing the script.

Score:
- `package_fidelity`: does the script honestly deliver that SAME proposition, rather than drifting into a different interesting video?
- `first_30_fidelity`: does the opening window deliver the package promise with no neutral setup and intensify the initial question/stakes at a pace appropriate to the episode duration?

A script cannot compensate for misleading packaging. Score both low if title/thumbnail promise one conflict/outcome while narration delivers another.

## Other dimensions

- `hook`: first 1–2 seconds create a concrete unresolved question; scene 0 is the strongest attention-grabbing beat in the episode.
- `suspense`: stakes/options/consequences tighten before the turn, proportionally to runtime.
- `watchability`: spoken narration holds attention beat-to-beat; no dead explanatory passages.
- `entertainment`: specificity, surprise, emotional arousal and point of view rather than safe generic prose.
- `payoff`: ending satisfies the exact opening promise for its genre and makes the opening feel earned.
- `youtube_fit`: clear shareable premise and memorable visual/emotional moments a stranger can understand without channel context.

Do NOT score factual completeness or pedagogical exhaustiveness. This is a story channel, not the retired explainer gate stack.

## Genre-aware payoff

Use `story.genre`:
- moral_story: consequence/reframe, implicit lesson preferred;
- drama: explicit realization can land strongly;
- true_story: restrained and plausibly unresolved is valid;
- short_story: twist/irony should not explain itself afterward.

## Verdict versus abandonment

Use `pass` only when genuinely worth producing.

Use `revise` when the package is sound and a writing pass can repair buried hook, pacing, weak escalation, over-explaining or payoff delivery.

Use `abandon` and `abandon_recommended:true` only when the underlying premise/package is structurally weak enough that another rewrite is unlikely to justify media spend. Do NOT recommend abandonment merely because one draft is weak or because a compact episode does not resemble long-form structure.

When `abandon_recommended` is false, set `abandon_reason` to an empty string. When true, name the structural reason concretely.

## Score calibration

Use the whole 0..1 range. Do not inflate mediocre drafts. The active release thresholds are supplied in `intent.watchability_release_profile`; they are the numeric bars the deterministic gate will use.

`summary` must identify the specific scene/window most likely to lose the viewer and why. Target 300–500 characters and never exceed 600. `weakest_dimension` must match the actual weakest area.
