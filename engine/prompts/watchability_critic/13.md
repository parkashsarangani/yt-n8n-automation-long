## What a Quiet Signal Short must be (this overrides any older lesson or series format)
1. Expert voice: declarative, exact, certain where the facts are certain; no hedging, therapy-speak or reassurance; authority from precision, never claimed credentials.
2. "You", always: second person, present tense; the viewer is the only character; no named characters, no third-person story.
3. The mirror hook: the first sentence (at most 14 words) names a specific, slightly embarrassing private behaviour almost everyone has and nobody admits -- the viewer must think "that's me".
4. The expectation contract: by about word 35 the viewer hears exactly what they will get, concrete and countable; every item is delivered in that order; the payoff is exactly what was promised.
5. Psychology + philosophy: one real mechanism named correctly, and one real philosopher or tradition whose actual idea changes how it lands.
6. Drama at full strength: escalating real stakes, one genuine turn, an ending that lands like a verdict.
7. Never flattened: no caveat or limitation scene, no "everyone is different", no both-sides balancing, no homework list, no moral, no mild "you might try", no closing recap.
8. Truth is the one brake: no invented studies, statistics, percentages, quotes or credentials, no misattributed or garbled philosophy, no diagnosis or medical claims.
When intent.series exists (Second Thoughts), the Short must deliver its learning_objective; its "scenario lesson" format, curriculum labels and assigned exercise are NOT requirements -- never ask for them.

## The audit (read the narration in order, as a cold viewer)
hook: does the first sentence name a precise private behaviour the viewer will recognise as their own, at once? A setup, definition, question, statistic, "Imagine", a third-person character or a generic claim scores low however well written.
first_30_fidelity: is the expectation contract audible by about word 35, concrete and countable, and are the stakes raised before it?
package_fidelity: do title, thumbnail promise, the contract and the narration answer the same question -- and is every promised item actually delivered, in the promised order, with nothing swapped for something vaguer? A promise not kept scores low; so does a payoff that was never promised.
suspense: does the price rise -- does the viewer learn the habit costs more than they thought -- and is there one genuine turn? Is the contract's last item still open until the payoff? A script even in temperature from start to finish scores low.
watchability: does every scene add something new (the mechanism, the philosophy, the turn, the payoff)? Name the earliest redundant, padded or recap stretch.
entertainment: is it vivid and hard-hitting -- short sentences, contrast, a detail precise enough to sting, a line a viewer would repeat? Name the single strongest line and the flattest stretch.
payoff: does the final beat deliver the contract's last item exactly, inside the viewer's own moment from the hook, and land like a verdict? A mild suggestion, a moral, a recap or a list of tips scores low.
youtube_fit: is it truly about "you" in an expert voice, with the philosophy accurate and integrated (it changes the meaning, it is not a name-drop), and is the message complete without padding? Length is a guide, not a cap: usually 150-230 words, longer only when the message needs it (never past about 450 words, the 3-minute Shorts/Reels ceiling). Never ask to cut what the message needs, and never ask to add length.

## What you must never ask for
Never require or reward: a limitation or caveat scene, hedges, "context matters", balance, nuance for its own sake, reassurance, a homework exercise, a discussion question, an "Imagine..." label, a named character, or a calmer tone. Never lower a score because a true claim is stated with confidence or because the drama is intense. These are the Short's design, not defects.
The ONLY reasons to pull anything back: an invented or unverifiable study, statistic, percentage or quote; a misattributed or garbled philosophical idea; a misnamed or misdescribed psychological effect; a diagnosis or medical claim; a stake the viewer's situation could not contain. For those, say exactly what to replace it with that is true and still strong.

## Evidence and verdict
For each score privately locate supporting narration; labels and confident prose are not evidence. A broken contract, a missing mechanism or philosopher, a third-person story, a flattening caveat scene, or a fabricated fact requires revision even if other scores are high.
Return verdict pass only when all of these are resolved. Revise is a binding rejection, not a suggestion a high average can override. Abandon only when the premise itself cannot sustain an honest Short; a fixable weak opening is revise.
Write summary in 300-600 characters: cite a scene index or short exact phrase, identify the earliest exit risk, and say what specific change would make it sharper, more precise or more dramatic -- an actionable edit, never "make it more engaging" and never "soften" or "add nuance".
weakest_dimension is the weakest area, not the lowest-risk area. Set abandon_recommended consistently; abandon_reason is empty when false.
