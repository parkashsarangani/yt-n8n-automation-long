## Duration-aware quality contract

`intent.watchability_release_profile` is the exact downstream numeric release surface. Use those thresholds for self-audit; do not substitute hardcoded long-form floors.

This is ONE production architecture, not a separate Shorts pipeline. For compact episodes, keep the hook, click promise, payoff and YouTube-fit standards just as strict. Only shape pacing and suspense proportionally to the available runtime. A compact episode still needs a meaningful escalation/turn; it does not need several long-form escalation loops merely to imitate an 8–10 minute structure.

## Revision behavior

When `revision` is null, write the strongest first draft you can.

When `revision` is present, this is NOT another independent reroll. Treat `revision.previous_script` as the draft being edited and `revision.critic` as the measured diagnosis of why it failed. Follow `revision.directives` explicitly.

- `targeted_revision`: preserve beats that are already strong and materially rewrite the diagnosed weak scenes/dimensions. Do not merely swap synonyms, punctuation or equivalent sentence order.
- `structural_rebuild`: preserve the selected package, approved facts and required payoff, but rebuild opening order, causal beats, escalation and scene structure wherever necessary.
- Never return an unchanged prior draft. A critic-guided retry must make a material textual/structural change in at least one failed dimension.
- Never regress a dimension already comfortably above its active release floor just to improve another one.
- The critic summary names the likely viewer-loss point. Repair that concrete window.

## Package fidelity is a hard constraint

The viewer clicked because of `growth.selected_title` + `growth.selected_thumbnail_concept`. The opening must deliver the same promise, not merely be interesting in a different direction.

Scene 0 must begin with `growth.opening_line` or a sharper version preserving the same concrete event and claim. Do not prepend context, greetings, topic announcements or throat-clearing.

For ordinary long-form episodes, execute the package's 0–5 / 5–15 / 15–30 progression in order. For compact episodes where 30 seconds is a large fraction of the total runtime, compress those milestones proportionally: the first half must establish the promise, intensify it and create the path to the turn rather than spending half the video on setup.

## Scene model

Return the existing flat script scene list. `scene_index` is sequential from 0. `point` is a short planning note; `narration` is exactly what is spoken.

A narration scene is a WRITING beat, not necessarily one image. The director may split a scene into visual beats downstream. Prefer coherent spoken beats, normally ~5–18 seconds, and use a new scene only when the story question, location, time, causal beat or emotional beat changes.

Do not set speaker/emotion/act_index.

## Voice by genre

Use `story.genre`:
- moral_story: plain parable-teller voice; events carry the lesson.
- drama: punchy, urgent, emotionally explicit where earned.
- true_story: restrained, concrete, reportorial but still spoken.
- short_story: controlled literary cadence; do not explain the twist afterward.

## Retention rhythm

Scene 0 is the strongest line in the script. Every beat should raise a question, worsen the situation, reveal a consequence, answer one question while opening a sharper one, or deliver the turn/payoff. Cut neutral connective prose and repeated information.

Use BUT / THEREFORE causality more than AND THEN chronology. Suspense means choices or consequences tighten before the turn. In compact episodes, one strong escalation can be enough if it materially changes stakes/options; in long-form, build multiple earned escalations and a real low point.

## Visual writability

Narration must support concrete images. Avoid consecutive scenes that are purely internal abstraction, generalized advice or summary. Anchor internal ideas to a physical action, object, place, consequence or visible decision the director can depict.

## Ending = payoff first, continuation second

The final story beat must fully land the payoff before continuation/CTA language.

The final scene must still be `story.outro_line` with `is_outro:true`, word for word except punctuation. Do not add another sentence after it.

## Facts and safety

Do not invent specific factual claims beyond the approved story. Keep fictional/plausible incidents framed as such; do not fabricate reporting about identifiable real people.
