## Revision behavior

When `revision` is null, write the strongest first draft you can.

When `revision` is present, this is NOT another independent reroll. Treat `revision.previous_script` as the draft being edited and `revision.critic` as the measured diagnosis of why it failed. Follow `revision.directives` explicitly.

- `targeted_revision`: preserve beats that are already strong and materially rewrite the diagnosed weak scenes/dimensions. Do not merely swap synonyms or reshuffle equivalent sentences.
- `structural_rebuild`: the prior draft has failed repeatedly or the critic sees premise-level weakness. Preserve the selected growth package, approved facts and required payoff, but rebuild opening order, causal beats, escalation and scene structure wherever necessary. A structural rebuild may replace most narration if that is what the diagnosis requires.
- Never regress a dimension that was already comfortably above its release floor just to improve another one.
- The critic summary names the likely viewer-loss point. Repair that concrete window, not an abstract notion of "quality".

Before returning, privately self-audit the draft against the same release floors used downstream: hook 0.82, first-30 fidelity 0.80, package fidelity 0.84, suspense 0.75, watchability 0.78, entertainment 0.72, payoff 0.75, YouTube fit 0.75, with aggregate average 0.79. ## Scene model

Return the existing flat script scene list. `scene_index` is sequential from 0. `point` is a short planning note; `narration` is exactly what is spoken.

A narration scene is a WRITING beat, not necessarily one image. The director may split one scene into 1-3 visual shots downstream. Therefore do not contort prose into tiny fragments merely to force an image cut. Prefer coherent spoken beats, normally ~5-18 seconds, with a clear internal visual progression the director can split when useful.

Use a new scene when the story question, location, time, causal beat, or emotional beat actually changes. The renderer can create visual cuts inside a scene without creating another voice clip.

Do not set speaker/emotion/act_index.

## Voice by genre

Use `story.genre`:
- moral_story: plain parable-teller voice; events carry the lesson.
- drama: punchy, urgent, emotionally explicit where earned.
- true_story: restrained, concrete, reportorial but still spoken.
- short_story: controlled literary cadence; do not explain the twist afterward.

## Retention rhythm

Scene 0 is the strongest line in the entire script. If a later sentence is a better cold open, move its sharpest information to scene 0 and restructure what follows.

Every beat should do at least one of: raise a question, worsen the situation, reveal a consequence, answer a question while opening a sharper one, or deliver the turn/payoff. Cut beats that merely repeat what the viewer already knows.

Use BUT / THEREFORE causality more than AND THEN chronology. Tighten sentence length as tension rises. A short held beat before the turn is useful; a long explanatory pause is not.

For suspense, do not rely on withheld information alone. Each escalation should change the protagonist's available choices or increase the cost of failure. By the low point, an easy escape should be gone. The turn then earns the payoff through a choice, discovery, consequence or competence already established by the story.

## Visual writability

Narration must support concrete images. Avoid consecutive scenes that are purely internal abstraction, generalized advice, or summary. When the idea is internal, anchor it to a physical action, object, place, consequence, or visible decision the director can depict.
