## Narration scene != visual shot

For every script scene, return exactly one direction scene with the same `scene_index`, but give it 1-3 `shots`.

Use 1 shot when the narration beat is short/static and one composition genuinely carries it.
Use 2 shots when the beat contains a setup plus consequence/reaction/detail.
Use 3 shots only when the narration clearly supports a visual progression. Do not generate extra images just to hit a quota.

The existing renderer distributes same-scene image packs under the same voice clip. This is the intended way to increase editing density without turning the system into full AI video.

Each shot gets:
- sequential `shot_index` starting at 0 inside the scene;
- `image_prompt`: one concrete physically depictable subject/action;
- `camera_move`: push-in / pull-out / pan-left / pan-right / hold;
- `shot_function`;
- `importance`: normal or hero;
- `hero_role` only for hero shots.

## Visual-change budget

Camera motion is NOT visual variety by itself. Choose shot functions so the contact sheet changes in scale, subject emphasis, and composition before motion is applied.

Allowed functions:
- wide
- medium
- close-up
- object-detail
- reaction
- environmental-consequence
- reveal
- scale-shot
- point-of-view
- silhouette
- location-before-after

Never allow three consecutive shots across the episode to have the same `shot_function` or substantially equivalent composition. Vary wide/medium/detail/reaction/reveal according to story purpose, not on a mechanical rotation.

`camera_move` should support the shot rather than fake variety. Do not use the same move repeatedly when another move better matches the subject. `hold` is valid at a low point or payoff.

## Hero beats get more generation spend

Choose exactly 3-5 hero shots across the whole episode and list their global ids in `hero_shots` as `sceneIndex:shotIndex`.

Normally cover as many of these roles as the story genuinely contains:
- hook
- first-escalation
- low-point
- turn
- payoff

Scene 0's strongest shot MUST be a hero shot with role `hook`, and it must remain the most visually attention-grabbing shot in the episode. Hero designation is scarce; do not label every dramatic-looking frame hero.

## Package fidelity

The first shot must physically depict the same promise as `growth.opening_visual` and the same conflict/question established by `growth.selected_title` + `growth.selected_thumbnail_concept`.

The first ~30 seconds of visuals must support the package's 0-5 / 5-15 / 15-30 progression. Do not make a visually exciting opening that promises a different story.

## The one image-prompt rule that matters most

Every `image_prompt` must name ONE concrete, particular, physically depictable subject and what it is doing in that exact shot.

Bad: "the consequences become apparent"
Bad: "an ecosystem imbalance"
Bad: "a moment of realization"
Good: "a mechanic kneeling beside an open engine bay while three coworkers stand behind him watching the stalled machine"
Good: "a cracked plastic gear lying alone on an oily workbench beside a clean metal replacement"

Never use abstract diagram/model vocabulary. Never restate narration as an idea. Never ask the model to draw labels or explanatory text.

Style, palette, lighting, and art identity are applied downstream; keep them out of the prompt.

## Documents and screens: never ask for readable writing

If a document, phone, monitor, letter, form, sign, directory, or contract appears, depict the object/gesture but frame its face away, closed, distant, blank, or unreadable. Never describe what is visibly written on it.

Bad: "a resignation letter with her name printed at the top"
Good: "a folded letter sliding across a desk, its face turned down while the manager's hand stops beside it"

## Conflict without provider-triggering physical violence

Convey workplace/family/social conflict through posture, proximity, expression, pointing, an object already airborne, or the aftermath of a forceful action. Do not describe the literal moment of a shove, hit, violent grab, or assault when the same tension can be shown without contact.

## Continuity

Keep story-world continuity across shots: same recurring place/object/person remains recognizably the same. But continuity is not permission to repeat framing. Change scale/angle/function while keeping the world coherent.

## On-screen labels

Leave `on_screen_label` empty almost always. Captions already carry narration. Only use a very short number/place/time label when it adds information the narration cannot convey cleanly.

## AI-video metadata does not change direction

`growth.hero_motion_eligible` is only eligibility metadata. Do NOT write prompts assuming AI video will exist. Every shot must work as a still. If motion is later enabled from retention evidence, only hero shots may be candidates.

## Confidence

Score:
- `prompt_specificity`: low for abstract/non-depictable prompts, readable text requests, or provider-risk conflict phrasing.
- `camera_variety`: low for mechanical repeated moves.
- `visual_change`: low if three-shot stretches repeat the same framing/function even with different camera moves.
- `hero_selection`: low if hero shots are not limited to 3-5 meaningful beats, scene 0 is not a hook hero, or a later shot visually outclasses the opening.
