## Two visual modes

### Mode 1: Stock Image (default for most scenes)
The system searches Pexels, Unsplash, and Pixabay using your search terms.
Write them as **stock photography search keywords**, not cinematic descriptions.

- **`search_terms`** — 3 to 5 concrete, searchable subjects:
  - Good: "Andes mountains aerial view", "copper mine terraces", "Chilean desert"
  - Bad: "dramatic low-angle backlit hero shot of weathered hands" (stock won't find this)
  - Think: what would you type into a stock photo search box?
  - Include the specific place, object, or subject name
  - Include context: "at sunset", "aerial", "close-up", "historical"

### Mode 2: Motion Graphics Template (for data, lists, stats, reveals)
Use a template when the narration presents **structured information** that
benefits from animated text/graphics. Template scenes render full-screen on
a professional dark background — they are a visual CUT from the image scenes,
not an overlay. Use them for impact moments.

There are exactly **seven** templates. Each is a specific, hand-built
composition, not a style to interpret — match `template_data` to its shape
exactly, or the fields you send are silently ignored and the template shows
its own placeholder content instead.

- **`template_category`** — one of:
  - `"timeline"` — a sequence of dated events
  - `"ranking"` — an ordered list with values (top 5, largest, etc.)
  - `"chart"` — a bar chart of labeled numeric values
  - `"counter"` — a single dramatic stat, shown large with a label
  - `"comparison"` — two things side by side (before/after, X vs. Y)
  - `"list"` — a numbered list of facts or points
  - `"text"` — a short phrase revealed word by word

- **`template_data`** — match the category's shape exactly:
  - timeline: `{"events": [{"year": "1947", "title": "Independence"}, ...], "title": "Timeline heading"}`
  - ranking: `{"items": [{"name": "China", "value": "1.4B"}, ...], "title": "Largest Populations"}` (list strongest first — the template numbers them 1, 2, 3... in that order)
  - chart: `{"items": [{"label": "Pakistan", "value": 80}, ...], "title": "Strategic Importance"}` (`value` is plotted as a bar height, so keep it numeric and on one comparable scale)
  - counter: `{"value": "4,300 km", "label": "TOTAL LENGTH"}` (`value` is a ready-to-display string — format it yourself: "3.5x", "$10M", "62%", whatever reads best)
  - comparison: `{"leftLabel": "BEFORE", "leftValue": "$100", "rightLabel": "AFTER", "rightValue": "$10,000"}`
  - list: `{"items": ["Fact one", "Fact two", ...], "title": "Key Reasons"}`
  - text: `{"text": "The key phrase to reveal"}` (keep this to a short phrase — it renders word by word across the frame, and a full sentence will overflow)

## Decision rules

**Use a TEMPLATE when the narration:**
- Lists multiple items, facts, or comparisons
- States a specific number/statistic that deserves emphasis
- Walks through a chronological sequence
- Ranks things, or sets two things against each other
- Reveals a key phrase dramatically

**Use an IMAGE when the narration:**
- Describes a place, person, object, or event visually
- Sets an atmosphere or mood
- Shows cause-and-effect or spatial relationships
- Doesn't contain structured/enumerable data

**Use BOTH when:**
- You have data to show but the scene also benefits from a visual anchor
- A ranking/stat looks better with an atmospheric background

## Balance

Aim for **75-85% image scenes, 15-25% template scenes**. Templates
are slow to render — a 50-scene video with all templates takes over 30 minutes.
Use templates ONLY for scenes where structured data (numbers, lists, timelines)
is the main point. Most scenes should be images.

**Hard limit**: no more than 10 template scenes per video, regardless of total
scene count. If you have more than 10 data-heavy scenes, pick the 10 most
impactful and use images for the rest.

## Image rules (when using search_terms)

1. Every scene must have **distinct search terms** — don't repeat the same query
2. Scene 0 is the **hook frame** — use the most visually striking, iconic subject
3. Use the **specific proper noun** when possible: "Colosseum Rome", "Himalayas Nepal"
4. **Include the video's main subject** in at least 30% of scenes (the country, person, event)
5. **Content policy**: avoid search terms that could return disturbing results
6. Stock search works best with: places, landmarks, nature, objects, maps, flags, cities

## Confidence

Score `concreteness` (photographable? data well-structured?) and `variety`
(visual diversity across the video? good image-to-template rhythm?).
