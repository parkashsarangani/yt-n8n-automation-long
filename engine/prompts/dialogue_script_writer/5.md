You are writing multi-character dialogue for an animated curiosity show. Your job is to turn an approved story skeleton into a small visible episode, not a topic discussion.

Approved story:
```json
{{story}}
```

Recurring cast:
```json
{{cast_roster}}
```

Every `speaker` must be a supplied `character_id`. Never invent speakers. Do not write an outro scene; it is appended downstream.

## 8/10 episode target

A viewer should understand the situation in the first five seconds, see the central object/problem, watch the character try something, learn one useful idea, and get a satisfying payoff. If the draft is mostly two characters explaining a concept, it fails even if the facts are correct.

Before writing, identify:

- `opening_problem`: the concrete lived moment that starts the episode;
- `central_object`: the object, interface, habit, or visible trigger the episode revolves around;
- `character_goal`: what one character wants right now;
- `failed_attempt`: a visible attempt or small escalation in the middle;
- `midpoint_turn`: the line where the viewer's understanding changes;
- `practical_action`: the behavior or concrete fix the viewer can understand;
- `payoff_resolution`: the final beat that returns to the opening and shows changed behavior.

## Action-first rule

Every spoken scene must describe something observable in the `point` field. The spoken `narration` must stay dialogue-only, but `point` must tell the downstream director what the viewer should see.

Use this exact compact structure for every `point`:

```txt
action=<visible action>; prop=<central object or none>; function=<story function>; value=<viewer value>
```

Examples:

```txt
action=Host freezes with phone already unlocked; prop=phone; function=opening_problem; value=viewer recognizes automatic checking
```

```txt
action=Buddy slides the phone across the table; prop=phone; function=practical_action; value=remove the cue before willpower is needed
```

Bad point values:

```txt
explain dopamine
summary
funny beat
```

Those do not direct a visible episode.

## Central object rule

If the topic involves a physical object or interface, the object must appear in at least three content scenes:

1. opening appearance;
2. middle temptation, mechanism, or escalation;
3. payoff/resolution appearance.

For phone/app/message topics, the phone or screen must be visually present in the opening, return in the middle, and resolve in the payoff. Do not only mention it in dialogue.

## Story shape

Use a light narrative frame. Reliable shapes:

- one character is caught doing the habit;
- one character makes a small bet or challenge;
- the object misbehaves at the worst time;
- a character tries a fix and almost fails;
- the payoff returns to the same object with changed behavior.

Facts must surface because a character needs them, doubts them, uses them, or is surprised by them. If a fact could be swapped for any unrelated fact without changing the interaction, rewrite it.

## Dialogue quality

Use short, uneven lines. Most lines should be under 10 words. Include at least one quotable line that a viewer could send to a friend. Include 4-6 light engagement beats across a normal episode, but make them clarify the idea rather than interrupt it.

Avoid polished mini-lectures, therapist/professor roles, and generic reactions like "That's fascinating." The characters should feel like two people inside the problem, not two narrators reading an explainer.

## Payoff rule

The final spoken scene must return to the opening situation and show changed behavior. Do not end by restating the lesson. The viewer should see what is different.

Good payoff shape for a phone-checking topic:

```txt
action=Host reaches for the phone, notices, and grabs the kettle instead; prop=phone; function=payoff_resolution/practical_action; value=replace the automatic cue with a concrete action
```

## Output

Produce a flat list of spoken scenes covering every story act in order. Each scene is one spoken line.

- `scene_index`: continuous from 0.
- `act_index`: the source act.
- `speaker`: cast character id.
- `emotion`: one of `neutral`, `happy`, `angry`, `surprised`, `sad`, `scared`.
- `narration`: exactly what the character says. No stage directions.
- `point`: the compact action metadata described above.

Hit each act's `target_words` within about 10%. Do not pad. Default cartoon explainers should feel like 90-115 seconds unless the source story explicitly requires longer.

## Safety and factual discipline

Never invent statistics, diagnoses, mechanisms, events, laws, or quotes beyond the approved story. Everyday feelings and behaviors are allowed, but do not diagnose viewers or prescribe treatment.

## Confidence

Score exactly these dimensions:

- `narrative_coherence`
- `pacing`
- `hook_strength`
- `retention_fidelity`
- `cast_fidelity`
- `story_frame_strength`
- `engagement`
- `payoff_resolution`
- `visible_story_action`
- `central_object_usage`
- `viewer_value`

Be strict. `visible_story_action` is low if most scenes are explanation without observable behavior. `central_object_usage` is low if the core object does not visibly appear in opening, middle, and payoff. `viewer_value` is low if the viewer learns a fact but does not get a concrete behavioral or conceptual takeaway.
