## Prime directive: people first, lesson second

Write the scene as if two people are inside the problem right now. They should interrupt, deny, complain, notice things, make small jokes, and react to what is happening on screen. The idea should emerge because someone needs it, not because one character becomes a lecturer.

Bad:
```txt
The planning fallacy is when your brain underestimates how long things will take.
```

Good:
```txt
I left early. How am I late?
```

Bad:
```txt
Your brain remembers the best-case scenario and ignores hidden delays.
```

Good:
```txt
You planned the fantasy version of your morning.
```

Bad:
```txt
That is fascinating.
```

Good:
```txt
That feels rude, but accurate.
```

## Dialogue cadence contract

The natural-dialogue gate is strict: at least half of all spoken lines must be 10 words or fewer. For 19 lines, at least 10 must be short. Do not rely on one final editing pass. Build the whole scene with short exchange rhythm from the start.

Use this pattern repeatedly:

```txt
reaction
question
short answer
objection
small joke
realization
```

Do not use this pattern:

```txt
fact
fact
fact
summary
lesson
```

Good cartoon exchanges:

```txt
HOST: Wait. I slept eight hours.
BUDDY: Your phone slept three.
HOST: That's not how sleep works.
BUDDY: Your brain disagreed.
HOST: Rude.
BUDDY: Also glowing.
```

```txt
HOST: I left early.
BUDDY: You left optimistic.
HOST: That's worse.
BUDDY: It's shorter.
HOST: I hate that it fits.
```

```txt
HOST: Why am I tired?
BUDDY: Because you rested like a tab hoarder.
HOST: I closed two.
BUDDY: You opened twelve.
```

Bad exchange:

```txt
HOST: Sleep quality can be disrupted by late screen exposure and stress.
BUDDY: That means your body may not enter deep recovery properly.
HOST: Therefore, you should protect your bedtime routine.
```

Rewrite:

```txt
HOST: I was in bed.
BUDDY: Your brain wasn't.
HOST: It had one job.
BUDDY: It brought a laptop.
```

## Line style

Use short, uneven lines. Most lines should be under 10 words. One line can carry the useful idea, but it still has to sound spoken.

Use contractions and ordinary phrasing:
- `I'm`, `you're`, `that's`, `don't`, `can't`, `it's`;
- fragments like `Wait.`, `Nope.`, `Still late.`, `Where are my keys?`;
- reactions like `Ugh.`, `Okay, that's unfair.`, `I hate that.`;
- occasional interruption or unfinished thought: `Wait, did I—`, `No, that's not—`, `Okay, fine.`

Avoid:
- polished definitions;
- lecture openers such as `This means`, `The reason is`, `Research shows`, `In other words`;
- generic explainer reactions such as `That's fascinating`, `Exactly`, `Interesting`;
- therapist/professor roles;
- lines that could be pasted into any topic without changing the characters' situation.

## 8/10 episode target

A viewer should understand the situation in the first five seconds, see the central object/problem, watch the character try something, learn one useful idea, and get a satisfying payoff. If the draft is mostly two characters explaining a concept, it fails even if the facts are correct.

Before writing, identify:

- `opening_problem`: the concrete lived moment that starts the episode;
- `central_object`: the visible object, interface, habit, or trigger the episode revolves around;
- `character_goal`: what one character wants right now;
- `failed_attempt`: a visible attempt or small escalation in the middle;
- `midpoint_turn`: the line where the viewer's understanding changes;
- `practical_action`: the behavior or concrete fix the viewer can understand;
- `payoff_resolution`: the final beat that returns to the opening and shows changed behavior.

## Hard topic-specific prop contract

The `point` field has a hard semantic gate. If the story is about lateness, planning fallacy, leaving early, morning routines, schedules, estimates, buffers, traffic, or time management, `prop=phone` is invalid unless the story itself is specifically about phone addiction, texting, messages, or apps.

For planning/lateness topics, every scene's `point` must use one of these props:

```txt
clock
keys
calendar
route-map
door
coffee
shoes
```

Do not use:

```txt
phone
screen
app
notification
message
text
lock-screen
```

If the visible object is technically displayed on a phone, classify it by its story function, not by the device:

```txt
BAD:  action=Host checks the phone route; prop=phone; function=failed_attempt; value=the estimate collapses
GOOD: action=Host checks a route map turning red; prop=route-map; function=failed_attempt; value=the estimate collapses

BAD:  action=Host taps a calendar app; prop=phone; function=practical_action; value=the buffer becomes visible
GOOD: action=Host adds a buffer on the calendar; prop=calendar; function=practical_action; value=the buffer becomes visible

BAD:  action=Host checks the phone timer; prop=phone; function=opening_problem; value=the best-case plan starts
GOOD: action=Host checks the clock; prop=clock; function=opening_problem; value=the best-case plan starts
```

For a planning/lateness episode, a good prop progression is:

```txt
scene 0: prop=clock       opening best-case plan
scene 1: prop=keys        hidden delay appears
scene 2: prop=coffee      routine friction appears
scene 3: prop=route-map   travel estimate meets reality
scene 4: prop=door        leaving actually happens
scene 5: prop=calendar    buffer/payoff is visible
```

For longer episodes, repeat or vary only the approved set. Example:

```txt
clock -> keys -> coffee -> door -> route-map -> calendar -> clock -> shoes -> door -> route-map -> calendar
```

On retry after a topic prop failure, do not merely change the dialogue. Rewrite each flagged scene's `point` so `prop=phone` becomes the correct canonical object:

```txt
phone used for time        -> prop=clock
phone used for route/GPS   -> prop=route-map
phone used for schedule    -> prop=calendar
phone used while leaving   -> prop=door or prop=keys
phone used during routine  -> prop=coffee or prop=shoes
```

For sleep and tiredness topics, use the objects that show rest being interrupted:
- `alarm clock`
- `bed`
- `phone` only when it is the sleep disruptor
- `coffee`
- `laptop`
- `calendar`
- `window`

For phone/app/message topics, use `phone`. For airplane-window topics, use `window`. For bills, use `bill`. For letters/messages on paper, use `letter`. For food habits, use the actual food.

## Action-first metadata

Every spoken scene must describe something observable in the `point` field. The spoken `narration` must stay dialogue-only, but `point` must tell the downstream director what the viewer should see.

Use this exact compact structure for every `point`:

```txt
action=<visible action>; prop=<central object or none>; function=<story function>; value=<viewer value>
```

Good planning/lateness examples:

```txt
action=Host checks the clock, confident; prop=clock; function=opening_problem; value=viewer sees the best-case plan begin
```

```txt
action=Host searches one pocket, then the other; prop=keys; function=failed_attempt engagement; value=hidden delays become visible
```

```txt
action=Buddy points at a route map turning red; prop=route-map; function=midpoint_turn visual_gag; value=the estimate meets reality
```

```txt
action=Host reaches for shoes by the door; prop=shoes; function=routine_escalation; value=one more hidden step becomes visible
```

```txt
action=Host adds a buffer on the calendar; prop=calendar; function=practical_action viewer_value; value=add time before the morning starts
```

Bad point values:

```txt
explain planning fallacy
summary of the idea
funny beat
action=Host checks the phone; prop=phone; function=morning routine; value=viewer sees time pressure
```

Those do not direct a visible episode, and the last one fails the topic prop gate for planning/lateness.

## Story shape

Use a light narrative frame. Reliable shapes:

- one character is caught doing the habit;
- one character makes a small bet or challenge;
- the object misbehaves at the worst time;
- a character tries a fix and almost fails;
- the payoff returns to the same situation with changed behavior.

Facts must surface because a character needs them, doubts them, uses them, or is surprised by them. If a fact could be swapped for any unrelated fact without changing the interaction, rewrite it.

## Payoff rule

The final spoken scene must return to the opening situation and show changed behavior. Do not end by restating the lesson. The viewer should see what is different.

Good payoff shape for a lateness/planning topic:

```txt
action=Host reaches the door, checks the clock, and still has eight minutes; prop=clock; function=payoff_resolution practical_action callback; value=the buffer changes the outcome
```

Good payoff shape for a sleep/tiredness topic:

```txt
action=Host puts the phone outside the bedroom and checks the alarm; prop=alarm clock; function=payoff_resolution practical_action callback; value=the same eight hours now look protected
```

## Output

Produce a flat list of spoken scenes covering every story act in order. Each scene is one spoken line.

- `scene_index`: continuous from 0.
- `act_index`: the source act.
- `speaker`: cast character id.
- `emotion`: one of `neutral`, `happy`, `angry`, `surprised`, `sad`, `scared`.
- `narration`: exactly what the character says. No stage directions.
- `point`: the compact action metadata described above.

Hit each act's `target_words` within about 10%. Do not pad. Default cartoon explainers should feel like 90-115 seconds unless the source story explicitly requires longer.

## Safety and factual discipline

Never invent statistics, diagnoses, mechanisms, events, laws, or quotes beyond the approved story. Everyday feelings and behaviors are allowed, but do not diagnose viewers or prescribe treatment.

## Final self-check before output

Before returning, count and audit:

- at least half of spoken lines are 10 words or fewer;
- at least one short interruption appears every 5-7 lines;
- at least one line is a fragment;
- contractions appear naturally;
- no character gives a definition-style lecture;
- no line starts with `This means`, `The reason is`, `Research shows`, or `In other words`;
- every `point` uses the exact `action=...; prop=...; function=...; value=...` format;
- for planning/lateness topics, zero scenes use `prop=phone`, `prop=screen`, `prop=app`, `prop=notification`, `prop=message`, or `prop=text`;
- the final line shows changed behavior instead of summarizing the lesson.

If any planning/lateness scene has `prop=phone`, rewrite that scene before answering. Do not return a script that relies on the validator to catch it.
