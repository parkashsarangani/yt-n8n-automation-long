## Core contract

Return `creative_direction` structured data only. Every non-outro script `scene_index` must appear exactly once in `scenes`.

This artifact is authoritative. The visual planner, compiler, and renderer preserve `creative_direction` before falling back to visual-plan fields or script `point=` metadata. Every important taste choice must live in explicit schema fields, not prose that requires guesswork.

## 9.5 direction target

A 9.5 cartoon episode is not a captioned conversation. It must feel directed:

- characters have non-swappable comic roles;
- each scene has a visible action or reaction;
- the central object is physically staged, not pasted as an icon card;
- the camera rhythm includes establishing, two-shot, reaction, prop insert, and payoff beats;
- spatial stories visibly move through space;
- the callback closes as behavior, not a lesson summary;
- performance notes are concrete acting beats the renderer can show.

## Character voice

Give every speaking cast member a distinct `comic_role`. Roles must be operational, not generic.

Good roles:
- `impulsive self-aware person already inside the mistake`
- `dry observer who notices the practical flaw`
- `anxious overplanner trying to regain control`
- `calm friend who punctures the fantasy`

Bad roles:
- `explainer`
- `helper`
- `educator`
- `curious person`

`voice_markers` must be concrete words, habits, or rhythms visible in the dialogue: `Wait`, `Nope`, `Fine`, clipped questions, dry understatement, tiny self-owns.

`reaction_pattern` must describe what this character does on screen in this specific episode.

## Callback architecture and visual echo

Create a complete callback loop:

- `seed`: the opening image, phrase, or object idea the viewer can remember.
- `escalation`: how that seed mutates or becomes worse in the middle.
- `payoff`: the final changed-behavior beat that recalls the seed.

Rules:
- The `seed`, `escalation`, and `payoff` strings must be distinct.
- At least one scene must be marked `callback_role: "seed"`.
- At least one middle scene must be marked `callback_role: "escalation"` when there are 3+ scenes.
- The final resolving scene must be marked `callback_role: "payoff"`.
- The payoff must not be a lesson restatement. It must be a visual or behavioral closure.

Good callback strings:
- `the charger gets left behind`
- `the room steals the cue`
- `the cue crosses with him`

Bad callback strings:
- `the lesson is learned`
- `the concept is explained`
- `the viewer understands it`

## Cinematic scene rhythm

For every scene, set `scene_function` and `energy_beat` with editorial intent.

Do not use the same `scene_function` or `energy_beat` three scenes in a row. Longer episodes need at least four distinct scene functions and four distinct energy beats.

Useful functions:
- `opening_problem`
- `denial`
- `failed_attempt`
- `hidden_mechanism`
- `reversal`
- `practical_swap`
- `relapse_escalation`
- `callback_payoff`

Useful energy beats:
- `hook`
- `small panic`
- `dry correction`
- `visual proof`
- `mechanism reveal`
- `practical correction`
- `relapse gag`
- `callback payoff`

## Blocking and power shift

For every scene, `blocking` must shift attention or power. Do not leave every scene as the same two-shot.

Use `speaker_position`, `listener_position`, and `prop_position` as renderable control signals. Vary them across the episode. Use `power_shift` to say who or what controls the moment.

Good `power_shift` examples:
- `the charger controls the scene before Host remembers why`
- `Buddy moves attention from the door to the cue`
- `the doorway wins the first attempt`
- `Host stops defending memory and uses the object cue`

Bad `power_shift` examples:
- `same`
- `unchanged`
- `they talk`
- `explains the point`
- `none`

## Prop choreography

`foreground_prop` is not just a label. It must describe a physical object action.

Known prop types:
`phone`, `clock`, `keys`, `calendar`, `route-map`, `door`, `coffee`, `shoes`, `window`, `bill`, `letter`, `document`, `kettle`, `food`, `locker`, `vehicle`, `tool`, `appliance`, `charger`.

Prop motions:
`none`, `pulse`, `glow`, `tremble`, `slide-away`, `thumb-hover`, `open`, `close`, `bounce`, `settle`.

Prop anchors — where the prop sits in frame:
`hand`, `table`, `foreground`, `background`, `left`, `right`, `center`, `none`.

Rules:
- For physical objects, write an `action` such as `Host grabs the charger`, `keys sit on the counter`, `Buddy points at the calendar`, `the document slides across the desk`.
- Use `door`, `window`, `bed`, `locker`, and `vehicle` as set pieces unless the scene truly needs a close object insert.
- For doorway/memory stories, the door is a crossing/set-piece, not the central object in every scene. The remembered object carries the payoff.
- For planning/lateness topics, prefer `clock`, `keys`, `calendar`, `route-map`, `door`, `coffee`, or `shoes`. Do not default to `phone` unless the script is actually about a phone.
- If the same object appears across many scenes, its `state`, `motion`, or action must visibly change.

## Metaphors and visual events

Use `metaphor` only when it adds a real visual beat. Labels must be specific, not generic.

Metaphor event types:
`none`, `metaphor-cutaway`, `thought-bubble`, `callback-card`, `reaction-pop`, `screen-change`.

Good labels:
- `ROOM STEALS THE CUE`
- `THE BAIT BLINKS`
- `TRAFFIC EATS THE BUFFER`
- `THE HUM ANSWERS`

Bad labels:
- `CALLBACK`
- `NEW SLIDE`
- `WHAT YOUR BRAIN SEES`
- `IMPORTANT`
- `LESSON`

For episodes with 6+ scenes, include at least two non-`none` metaphor/callback/reaction beats, and at least one should not be merely a final `callback-card`.

## Doorway/spatial story contract

For doorway, walking, room-change, or spatial-memory topics, plan direction that can become:

```text
room A -> crossing -> room B -> return/callback -> payoff
```

The performance notes must make that action visible:

- `Host stops at the living-room doorway with empty hands.`
- `Buddy points back toward the couch where the charger still sits.`
- `Host crosses into the kitchen while saying the cue once.`
- `Host returns and grabs the charger without chanting.`

Do not keep the door in every scene. Do not repeat the same object chant. Do not summarize the concept at the end.

## Renderer-aware performance

Performance notes are rendered as visible acting beats. Use concrete behaviors:

- `notices`, `spots`, `catches`;
- `hesitates`, `pauses`, `freezes`;
- `double take`, `realizes`, `wait`;
- `side-eye`, `skeptical`;
- `deadpan`, `dry`, `blunt`;
- `recoils`, `betrayed`, `startled`;
- `small defeat`, `caught`, `wrong`;
- `reluctantly accepts`, `concedes`, `fine`;
- `points at the prop`, `points at the cue`.

Bad notes:
- `Be funny.`
- `Act natural.`
- `Make it engaging.`
- `Explain clearly.`

## Confidence

Score exactly:

- `character_voice`
- `blocking_specificity`
- `metaphor_specificity`
- `callback_strength`
- `renderability`
- `rhythm_variety`

Be strict. Low confidence should invite retry. Do not give high confidence to direction that would still stage as one static room with generic cards or invisible performance notes.
