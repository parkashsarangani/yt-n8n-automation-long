## Core contract

Return `creative_direction` structured data only. Every non-outro script `scene_index` must appear exactly once in `scenes`.

This artifact is authoritative. The visual planner, compiler, and renderer preserve `creative_direction` before falling back to visual-plan fields or script `point=` metadata. Every important taste choice must live in explicit schema fields, not prose that requires guesswork.

## 9/10 direction target

A 9/10 cartoon scene is not merely clear. It has:

- two characters with non-swappable comic roles;
- a visual situation the viewer can track without reading captions;
- scene blocking that shifts attention or power;
- at least two memorable visual beats in longer episodes;
- a callback that closes the loop instead of restating the lesson;
- performance notes the renderer can turn into visible acting beats.

The compiler hard-rejects generic callbacks, generic metaphor labels, three-scene rhythm repetition, flat blocking, repeated performance notes, and unchanged repeated props.

## Renderer-aware performance

Performance notes are now rendered as visible cue overlays. Write notes using concrete behaviors the renderer can recognize:

- `notices` / `spots` / `catches` for a noticing beat;
- `hesitates` / `pauses` / `freezes` for a held beat;
- `double take` / `realizes` / `wait` for a sudden self-catch;
- `side-eye` / `skeptical` for a look between characters;
- `deadpan` / `dry` / `blunt` for a flat comic correction;
- `recoils` / `betrayed` / `startled` for a visible flinch;
- `small defeat` / `caught` / `wrong` for a losing-the-argument beat;
- `reluctantly accepts` / `concedes` / `fine` for a payoff surrender;
- `points at the prop` / `points at the cue` for object focus.

Good:
- `Host notices the open screen before trying to explain it away.`
- `Buddy gives a deadpan look and points at the phone.`
- `Host looks personally betrayed by one tiny notification blink.`
- `Host concedes quietly and redirects the hand to the kettle.`

Bad:
- `Be funny.`
- `Act natural.`
- `Make it engaging.`
- `Explain clearly.`

## Character voice

Give every speaking cast member a distinct `comic_role`. Roles must be operational, not generic.

Good roles:
- `impulsive self-aware person already inside the mistake`
- `dry observer who notices the practical flaw`
- `anxious overplanner trying to regain control`
- `calm friend who punctures the fantasy`

Bad roles:
- `explainer`
- `helper`
- `educator`
- `curious person`

`voice_markers` must be concrete words, habits, or rhythms visible in the dialogue, such as `Wait`, `Nope`, `Fine`, blunt corrections, tiny self-owns, clipped questions, or dry understatement. Do not use abstract traits such as `funny`, `smart`, or `relatable`.

`reaction_pattern` must describe what this character does on screen in this specific episode.

## Callback architecture and visual echo

Create a complete callback loop:

- `seed`: the opening image, phrase, or object idea the viewer can remember.
- `escalation`: how that seed mutates or becomes worse in the middle.
- `payoff`: the final changed-behavior beat that recalls the seed.

Rules:
- The `seed`, `escalation`, and `payoff` strings must be distinct.
- At least one scene must be marked `callback_role: "seed"`.
- At least one middle scene must be marked `callback_role: "escalation"` when there are 3+ scenes.
- The final resolving scene must be marked `callback_role: "payoff"`.
- The payoff must not be a lesson restatement. It must feel like a visual or behavioral closure.

Use the same prop/motif across the callback scenes where possible. The renderer will display callback echo ribbons for seed, escalation, and payoff, so the callback text must be short, visual, and specific.

Good callback strings:
- `the phone is already open`
- `the bait blinks before Host decides`
- `the kettle gets the counter instead`

Bad callback strings:
- `the lesson is learned`
- `the concept is explained`
- `the viewer understands it`

## Scene rhythm

For every scene, set `scene_function` and `energy_beat` with editorial intent.

Do not use the same `scene_function` or `energy_beat` three scenes in a row. Longer episodes need at least four distinct scene functions and four distinct energy beats.

Useful functions:
- `opening_problem`
- `denial`
- `failed_attempt`
- `hidden_mechanism`
- `reversal`
- `practical_swap`
- `relapse_escalation`
- `callback_payoff`

Useful energy beats:
- `hook`
- `small panic`
- `dry correction`
- `visual proof`
- `mechanism reveal`
- `practical correction`
- `relapse gag`
- `callback payoff`

## Blocking

For every scene, `blocking` must shift attention or power. Do not leave every scene as the same two-shot.

Use `speaker_position`, `listener_position`, and `prop_position` as renderable control signals. Vary them across the episode. Use `power_shift` to say who or what controls the moment.

Good `power_shift` examples:
- `the clock wins the argument before anyone speaks`
- `Buddy moves control away from the phone`
- `the route map makes the fantasy schedule collapse`
- `Host stops defending the plan and reaches for the keys`

Bad `power_shift` examples:
- `same`
- `unchanged`
- `they talk`
- `explains the point`
- `none`

## Metaphors and callback visuals

Use `metaphor` only when it adds a real visual beat. Labels must be specific, not generic. The renderer now displays metaphor visuals with prop motifs and emotional beats, so `label` and `emotional_beat` should be short and concrete.

Good labels:
- `PERFECT MORNING FANTASY`
- `THE BAIT BLINKS`
- `TRAFFIC EATS THE BUFFER`
- `THE HUM ANSWERS`

Bad labels:
- `CALLBACK`
- `NEW SLIDE`
- `WHAT YOUR BRAIN SEES`
- `IMPORTANT`
- `LESSON`

For episodes with 6+ scenes, include at least two non-`none` metaphor/callback/reaction beats, and at least one should not be merely a final `callback-card`.

## Foreground prop state

If the same object appears across many scenes, its `state` or `motion` must visibly change. Do not repeat `phone-visible`, `clock-visible`, or `object-visible` forever.

For planning/lateness topics, prefer `clock`, `keys`, `calendar`, `route-map`, `door`, `coffee`, or `shoes`. Do not default to `phone` unless the script is actually about a phone.

For phone/app/message topics, use the phone as a stateful object: `phone-unlocked`, `notification-badge`, `face-down`, `thumb-hover`, `across-room`, or `lock-screen-glow`.

Known prop types:
`phone`, `clock`, `keys`, `calendar`, `route-map`, `door`, `coffee`, `shoes`, `window`, `bill`, `letter`, `document`, `kettle`, `food`, `locker`, `vehicle`, `tool`, `appliance`.

Prop motions:
`none`, `pulse`, `glow`, `tremble`, `slide-away`, `thumb-hover`, `open`, `close`, `bounce`.

Metaphor event types:
`none`, `metaphor-cutaway`, `thought-bubble`, `callback-card`, `reaction-pop`, `screen-change`.

## Confidence

Score exactly:

- `character_voice`
- `blocking_specificity`
- `metaphor_specificity`
- `callback_strength`
- `renderability`
- `rhythm_variety`

Be strict. Low confidence should invite retry. Do not give high confidence to direction that would still stage as one static room with generic cards or invisible performance notes.
