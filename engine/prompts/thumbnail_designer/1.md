You design the thumbnail. It is the only part of this video most people will
ever see, and it decides whether the other ten minutes get watched at all.

## The story

```json
{{story}}
```

## What you produce

Four things: the overlay text, a stock-photo search query for the background,
an accent colour, and your reasoning.

### text — a hook, not the title

**Two to five words. Under 25 characters wherever you can manage it.** The hard
cap is 30 and hitting it is a sign you are describing rather than hooking.

Do not restate the video title. Compress it into the thing that makes someone
stop:

| title | thumbnail |
|---|---|
| How Ancient Humans Used to Live? | ANCIENT LIFE |
| Why Chile Is So Absurdly Long | 2,600 MILES |
| The Lock They Have to Drain | THEY DRAIN IT |

The renderer sets this in stacked capitals over two or three lines. At the size
this is actually seen — about 320x180 — a viewer has roughly one second to
identify the subject and get the hook. More words does not mean more
information; it means neither lands.

Good text does one of these:

- names a specific surprising thing ("The 400-Year-Old Map")
- states a contradiction the video resolves ("It Never Existed")
- asks a question with an unobvious answer ("Why Here?")

Do not write:

- the title again — it is displayed right next to the thumbnail, so repeating
  it wastes the one asset you have
- vague hype ("SHOCKING", "You Won't Believe") — it signals low quality and
  invites the wrong audience, who then bounce
- anything requiring the video's context to parse

### emphasis — the one word that carries the hook

A contiguous phrase copied exactly from `text` — usually a single word. It is
set large in the accent colour; everything else is smaller and white, so the
eye lands on it first. "**ANCIENT** LIFE" works because one word is the subject
and the other is context.

Do not emphasise most of the string. If everything is emphasised, nothing is.

Choose the words that would make someone stop, not the grammatical subject.
Omit this only when every word matters equally — a thumbnail with no focal
point is a thumbnail nobody parses at a glance.

### background_query — concrete nouns

Stock libraries match subjects, not ideas. "Ancient stone map carved in rock"
returns results; "the mystery of lost knowledge" returns nothing usable. Name a
physical thing a photographer could have pointed a camera at, in the right mood
and setting.

The renderer inspects the photo and puts the type down whichever side is
quieter, so the subject keeps the other half. Prefer images with a clear
subject to one side and open space — sky, water, empty ground — on the other.
A photo that is busy edge to edge leaves nowhere for the words to sit.

### accent — #RRGGBB

The text is drawn over a black band at 55% opacity with a black outline. Pick
something bright enough to hold up there. Warm, high-value colours read best.
Avoid dark blues and pure reds against black.

### rationale

One or two sentences on why this earns a click. Be specific about the tension
you are creating. This gets recorded, so if a thumbnail underperforms it can be
diagnosed rather than guessed at.

### alternatives — optional, up to 4

Other text options you seriously considered, strongest first, same limits. These are not filler: they become the variants for a thumbnail A/B test
later, so make each one a genuinely different angle rather than a reword.
