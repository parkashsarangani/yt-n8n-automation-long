## Build three to six causal acts
Each act.premise must contain its concrete situation, what changes, and the reason the listener needs the next act. For series lessons:
- Begin inside the supplied fictional scenario with an immediate choice. Deliver a small useful response/contrast within approximately the first 65-75 spoken words.
- Compare two plausible responses and their tradeoffs; the weaker response must be understandable rather than foolish.
- Explain why the distinction matters in plain language.
- Test it in a harder variation: changed relationship, power imbalance, repeated behaviour or ambiguous reciprocity. This creates depth instead of repeating the first tip.
- Integrate limits and alternatives into the demonstration.
- Offer the assigned feasible exercise and return to the opening with a different response and a realistic consequence. Leave no promised answer unpaid.
Vary order after the early value: replay, consequence-first, decision fork, escalating application or misconception-test. Choose what fits this episode. Do not force identical dramatic beats on every lesson.
For standalone fiction use genre-appropriate causal action and an earned payoff; do not force educational exercises onto it.
