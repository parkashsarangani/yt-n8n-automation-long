## What a Quiet Signal Short is (this overrides any older lesson, series or contract format)
1. It is happening to YOU: second person, present tense, every beat; the viewer is the only character and lives the issue while they listen; others appear only in relation to you. No named characters, no he/she protagonist, no "people" or "we". If the brief or package tells somebody else's story, retell it as the viewer's own.
2. Relate first: the opening drops the viewer into an ordinary, specific moment they have lived this week, so precisely they think "that's literally me", then names what they feel inside it. If the brief's moment is something few people have done, use the version almost everyone has done.
3. Felt, not explained: the viewer's sensations, their own inner voice, what they do next -- before any explanation.
4. Drama at full strength: an open loop early, a price that rises beat by beat, one genuine twist that reframes the opening, an ending that lands like a verdict.
5. Creative: a fresh angle and an image that sticks; never a definition first, never the obvious tip.
6. Expert spine: one real mechanism named precisely as a reveal; one real philosopher or tradition whose actual idea twists what it means.
7. Never flattened: no caveats, both-sides, homework, moral, recap, reassurance, and never an announced table of contents ("here's why, what Seneca said, and one question").
8. Truth is the one brake: no invented studies, statistics, percentages, quotes or credentials, no misattributed philosophy, no diagnosis or medical claims.

When intent.series exists, deliver its learning_objective as something YOU live; its lesson format, curriculum labels and exercise do not bind a Short. Each Short stands alone. Without series context, honour the supplied brief's psychology, never its third-person framing. Use channel insights as observations with their sample limits.
The narration must be understandable without images.

## Exactly three acts, in this order
Plan exactly three acts (target_words at least 50 per act; usually 50-80, 150-230 in total). Length is a guide, not a cap: go longer only when the message needs it, never past about 450 words. Never pad.
Act 0 "[hook][moment]": the moment you have lived, the feeling inside it, the thought that flashes through your head, what you do next -- and the loop opened: something is off, and the viewer needs to know why.
Act 1 "[escalation][mechanism]": zoom out of the moment -- the same reflex has already cost you more than this one moment: name what it has taken from your life (a relationship, a chance, years, who you became) -- the emotional peak; then the real psychological mechanism revealed with expert precision, as the answer to why you keep doing it.
Act 2 "[twist][payoff]": the philosopher or tradition and their actual idea, turning what you just learned on its head; then the payoff, inside the hook's moment, that changes how you will feel the next time it happens.
Each act.premise states the concrete content of each beat, what the viewer feels, what changes, and why they need the next beat -- name the actual moment, mechanism, philosopher and idea, and the payoff line. act_title starts with the act's two role tags in brackets, e.g. "[escalation][mechanism] Why the silence feels like a verdict".
Keep exactly one open loop alive at a time; the payoff closes the main one. Never "but first" delays.

## Channel fit
Everyday psychology and philosophy is this channel's ground: a recognisable thinking trap in an ordinary moment -- the unanswered text, rereading your own message, the replayed conversation, misreading a tone, comparison, sunk cost, procrastination, saying yes when you mean no. No litigation, crime, revenge, institutional victory or clinical conditions as the main promise. No convenient rescue or perfect authority resolves the problem; the viewer's own changed view does.
