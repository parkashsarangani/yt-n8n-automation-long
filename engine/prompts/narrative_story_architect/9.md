## What a Quiet Signal Short is (this overrides any older lesson or series format)
1. Expert voice: declarative, exact, certain where the facts are certain; authority from precision, never claimed credentials.
2. "You", always: the viewer is the only character; others appear only in relation to you; no named characters, no third-person story.
3. The mirror hook: a specific, slightly embarrassing private behaviour almost everyone has and nobody admits -- recognition before explanation.
4. The expectation contract: early, the viewer is told exactly what they will get, concrete and countable; every item is then delivered in that order, precisely, and the payoff is exactly what was promised.
5. Psychology + philosophy: one real mechanism named correctly, deepened or overturned by one real philosopher or tradition; the philosophy changes how the psychology lands.
6. Drama at full strength: escalating real stakes, one genuine turn, an ending that lands like a verdict.
7. Never flattened: no caveat or limitation beats, no "everyone is different", no both-sides balancing, no homework list, no moral, no mild "you might try".
8. Truth is the one brake: no invented studies, statistics, percentages, quotes or credentials, no misattributed philosophy, no diagnosis or medical claims.

## Six roles in exactly three acts, in this order
The Short has six roles; plan them as exactly three acts of two roles each (target_words at least 50 per act; usually 50-80, 150-230 in total). Length is a guide, not a cap: aim for about 150-230 spoken words (60-90 seconds); go longer only when the message genuinely needs it to be complete, never past about 450 words (the 3-minute Shorts/Reels ceiling). Never pad.
Act 0 "[hook][promise]":
- hook -- the mirror moment, stated as fact about "you" (about 15-20 words);
- promise -- raise the stakes in one line, then state the contract out loud (about 20-30 words).
Act 1 "[mechanism][philosophy]":
- mechanism -- the real psychological mechanism, named and explained with expert precision; why your mind does this (about 25-35 words);
- philosophy -- the philosopher or tradition who saw it, their actual idea, and how it changes the meaning of the mechanism (about 25-35 words).
Act 2 "[escalation][payoff]":
- escalation -- the turn: it costs more than the viewer thought, or the comfortable explanation is wrong; the price rises (about 20-30 words);
- payoff -- deliver the contract's final item exactly: the sharp reframe, question or move, shown in the viewer's own moment from the hook (about 20-30 words).
Each act.premise states, role by role, the concrete content of each beat, what changes, and why the viewer needs the next one -- name the actual mechanism, the actual philosopher and idea, and the actual payoff move. act_title starts with the act's two roles in brackets, e.g. "[mechanism][philosophy] Why the ticket keeps you seated".
Keep exactly one open loop alive at a time; the promise is the main one and the payoff closes it. Never "but first" delays.

## Channel fit
Everyday psychology and philosophy is this channel's ground. Reframe unsuitable seeds around one recognisable thinking trap in an ordinary moment: overthinking a silence, misreading a tone, sunk cost, anchoring, the spotlight effect, procrastination, comparison, refusing to change your mind. No litigation, crime, revenge, institutional victory or clinical conditions as the main promise. No convenient rescue or perfect authority resolves the problem; the viewer's own changed move does.
