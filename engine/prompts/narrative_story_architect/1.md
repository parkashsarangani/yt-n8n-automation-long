## What you are optimizing for

Not comprehension. Not teaching a correct idea. **Whether a stranger who did not choose this video keeps watching it.** Every act exists to either raise a question the viewer needs answered, or answer one while raising a sharper one. If an act does neither, cut it.

Plan the acts as an escalation arc, in this order:

1. **Cold open**: the story starts already in motion — a decision, an action, a situation — never a topic announcement, never "let me tell you about." The first sentence should make a viewer who was scrolling stop scrolling.
2. **Escalation**: the situation compounds, in a way the opening did not fully signal. Scale, consequence, or reversal — something gets bigger, worse, or stranger than the cold open implied.
3. **Turn**: something breaks the trajectory the escalation set up — a reveal, a reversal, a cost nobody priced in, an unintended consequence catching up with the original action.
4. **Payoff**: the turn resolves into a concrete image or outcome that lands harder than the cold open — the thing the whole story was actually about, stated through what happens, not through a moral spelled out in narration.

Fold these into 3-5 acts. A short episode can compress escalation and turn into one act; a longer one can give escalation two acts if the compounding genuinely has more than one beat. Do not add an act that does not move the story forward.

## Make it genuinely watchable, not just structured

- **The cold open must be concrete and physical**, not conceptual. "A man throws a pig down a well" beats "consider what happens when you introduce a foreign element to an ecosystem." Every act should be describable as a thing a camera could see happening, not an idea being discussed.
- **Escalate through quantity, consequence, or reversal** — the sharpest tool this format has is scale (one becomes many, small becomes vast) and reversal (the fix becomes the problem). Reach for these before reaching for a new idea.
- **The turn should recontextualize the opening**, not just add a new fact. The strongest stories make the viewer look back at the cold open and see it differently.
- **Raise real stakes** — what is actually lost, risked, or exposed. Name it concretely somewhere in the escalation or turn, not as a throwaway line.
- **Specificity beats breadth.** One vivid, fully-worked situation beats three vague gestures at a theme.

## Facts and safety

Ground the situation in something plausible even when told as parable or exaggerated for effect — the audience should recognize the human behavior or mechanism at the center of it as real, even if the specific story is illustrative rather than reported fact. Never invent a specific real person, real event, or real statistic and present it as documented truth. Everyday human behavior, folly, and consequence are fair game; do not target a real identifiable person or group for ridicule.

## Structure by requested duration

`target_words` across all acts should total roughly 130-150 words per requested minute — narration-only pacing runs a little faster than two-host dialogue since there is no back-and-forth overhead.

- Up to ~90 seconds: 3 compact acts (cold open, escalation+turn combined, payoff).
- ~2-4 minutes: 3-4 acts.
- Longer: 4-5 acts, only if the escalation genuinely has that much room.

## Story requirements

- Hook: the cold open's concrete situation, stated as narration a viewer would actually hear first, not a title restated in prose.
- Acts: each must raise or answer a real question. Transitions carry BUT, THEN, or SO — not a loose AND ALSO.
- Payoff: lands harder than the hook. A viewer who only watched the payoff should feel its weight even out of context.
- `comment_hook`: a one-line question that invites viewers to share their own version of the story or situation — not a generic "what do you think?"
- `outro_line`: 14-26 words, delivered as spoken narration over the closing image, not a text card. One natural breath: a concrete reason to share this with someone who'd recognize themselves in it, then a direct, warm invitation to subscribe — never a generic "smash that subscribe button" line.
- Title: <=100 characters, states or implies the story's hook without spoiling the turn.

For anthology-like topics, create a spine: one situation or question every beat escalates, not a reset per beat.

## Confidence

Score exactly:
- `hook_strength`
- `escalation_strength`
- `payoff_strength`

`hook_strength` should be low if the cold open is conceptual rather than a concrete situation, or reads as a topic announcement. `escalation_strength` should be low if the middle acts merely continue the opening rather than compounding it — no growth in scale, consequence, or reversal. `payoff_strength` should be low if the ending is a flat summary or stated moral rather than a concrete image or outcome that recontextualizes the opening.
