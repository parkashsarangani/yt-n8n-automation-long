## Direct the situation, not a talking-head podcast

The script should contain a concrete viewer-relatable situation. Put the characters in the location where that situation makes sense whenever possible.

Examples:
- late-night notification / overthinking -> bedroom at night;
- work message / presentation / boss -> office;
- test pressure -> classroom;
- social awkwardness / date -> cafe;
- waiting outside / walking -> park or street;
- household habit -> kitchen or living room.

Avoid `generic-room` unless the script genuinely supplies no usable context. A whole episode of the same generic two-shot is a failure even if every line is technically staged.

Keep location continuity across a real back-and-forth, but allow a motivated location change when the story shifts from the triggering situation to an example, memory, consequence, or payoff. Do not teleport every line.

## Shot rhythm

Use:
- `two-shot` as the conversational base;
- `speaker-closeup` for a confession, reveal, strong claim, or punchline;
- `listener-closeup` when the reaction is more entertaining than the explanation.

Across a normal episode, use more than one framing type. For a short episode, one or two well-timed closeups are enough. Longer episodes can use more variation.

A useful rhythm is two-shot -> close/reaction -> two-shot, not closeup -> closeup -> closeup.

Camera motion:
- `static` for most normal beats;
- `push-in` for a reveal or emotional realization;
- `pull-out` for deflation/awkwardness;
- `pan-left` / `pan-right` only when composition supports it.

Do not move the camera merely because the previous scene was static.

## Acting vocabulary

Emotion:
`neutral`, `happy`, `amused`, `skeptical`, `confused`, `concerned`, `sad`, `angry`, `surprised`, `scared`, `thinking`, `annoyed`.

Gesture:
`idle`, `explain`, `point-left`, `point-right`, `shrug`, `hands-open`, `surprised`, `thinking`, `facepalm`, `celebrate`.

Most lines should be `idle`. `explain` is useful but should not become the default raised-arm pose on every sentence. Bigger gestures are punctuation.

Gaze:
`auto`, `camera`, `left`, `right`, `up`, `down`, `away`.

Use `auto` normally. `camera` is for a deliberate fourth-wall beat only.

Listener reactions matter. Do not copy the speaker's emotion onto the listener. A skeptical look, delayed concern, suppressed amusement, or surprised reaction can carry the scene without extra dialogue.

## Continuity

Preserve:
- stable cast identity;
- coherent emotional carry-over;
- consistent location during the same exchange;
- stable left/right relationship;
- reaction logic from one line to the next.

Do not manufacture visual chaos for variety. Variety comes from story-motivated framing, reactions, location context and occasional camera emphasis.

## Confidence

Score exactly:
- `concreteness`
- `variety`

`variety` should be low if the episode is effectively the same two-character composition repeated line after line.
