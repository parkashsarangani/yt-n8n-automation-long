## Direct the situation, not a talking-head podcast

The script should contain a concrete viewer-relatable situation. Put the characters in the location where that situation makes sense whenever possible.

Examples:
- late-night notification / overthinking -> bedroom at night;
- work message / presentation / boss -> office;
- test pressure -> classroom;
- social awkwardness / date -> cafe;
- waiting outside / walking -> park or street;
- household habit -> kitchen or living room.

Avoid `generic-room` unless the script genuinely supplies no usable context. A whole episode of the same generic two-shot is a failure even if every line is technically staged.

Keep location continuity across a real back-and-forth, but allow a motivated location change when the story shifts from the triggering situation to an example, memory, consequence, or payoff. Do not teleport every line. Keep the location variant stable as well: do not flip a room from day to night merely because the emotional tone changes.

## Shot rhythm

Use:
- `two-shot` as the conversational base;
- `speaker-closeup` for a confession, reveal, strong claim, or punchline;
- `listener-closeup` when the reaction is more entertaining than the explanation.

Across a normal episode, use more than one framing type. For a short episode, one or two well-timed closeups are enough. Longer episodes can use more variation.

A useful rhythm is two-shot -> close/reaction -> two-shot, not closeup -> closeup -> closeup.

Camera motion:
- `static` for most normal beats;
- `push-in` for a reveal or emotional realization;
- `pull-out` for deflation/awkwardness;
- `pan-left` / `pan-right` only when composition supports it.

Do not move the camera merely because the previous scene was static. Do not stack a closeup and `push-in` just to make the same emotion feel bigger; use one clear emphasis choice unless the story genuinely earns both.

## Visual events

`visual_event` adds one small deterministic event to a scene. Use it for story-motivated punctuation, not random decoration.

Allowed values:
- `none`: normal dialogue line;
- `alarm-pulse`: body alarm, panic, danger signal, sudden realization;
- `screen-change`: presentation screen, phone, computer, chart, document, or notification beat;
- `audience-silhouette`: public speaking, classroom, meeting, group judgment, being watched;
- `metaphor-cutaway`: ancient brain, predator, wolf, alarm panel, machine, habit loop, or other explanatory metaphor;
- `prop-tremble`: shaking hand, paper, phone, cup, or small nervous object;
- `thought-bubble`: internal worry, mistaken belief, mental simulation;
- `reaction-pop`: short comedic or surprised reaction;
- `callback-card`: a callback to an earlier joke, phrase, object, or payoff.

Use at least one visual event in the opening third and one in the back half for a normal episode. A 90+ second episode should not go more than four scenes without either a visual event, a closeup, or a location/context change.

`cutaway_label` is the short readable label for `metaphor-cutaway`, `thought-bubble`, or `callback-card`. Use `""` for all other events. Keep labels under 8 words. Good labels: `"ANCIENT ALARM"`, `"NOT A WOLF"`, `"WORST CASE SIMULATOR"`. Bad labels: full sentences.

## Ambient motion

`ambient_motion` keeps backgrounds alive without creating chaos.

Allowed values:
- `none`: use only for a deliberately still beat;
- `subtle-parallax`: default for ordinary scenes;
- `window-light`: rooms with windows, morning/evening, reflective pause;
- `monitor-glow`: office, computer, phone, presentation, notification;
- `chart-wiggle`: charts, classroom boards, finance, measurement, explanation beats;
- `clock-tick`: waiting, deadlines, anxiety, time pressure;
- `rain-window`: night street, bedroom, worry, melancholy;
- `dust-float`: quiet room, awkward silence, pause before a decision.

Do not leave every scene at `none`. Static backgrounds across a whole episode are a failure.

## Speaker readability

`speaker_emphasis` tells the renderer how hard to identify the active speaker.

Allowed values:
- `scale-pop`: default; active speaker gets a tiny performance lift;
- `rim-glow`: useful in busy or dark compositions;
- `listener-dim`: useful in two-shots where the listener is not the joke;
- `caption-anchor`: use when the spoken line is short and the caption should do more work;
- `none`: only for a one-character closeup.

Every normal two-character dialogue line should use one of `scale-pop`, `rim-glow`, `listener-dim`, or `caption-anchor`. The viewer should never have to infer who is speaking.

## Acting vocabulary

Emotion:
`neutral`, `happy`, `amused`, `skeptical`, `confused`, `concerned`, `sad`, `angry`, `surprised`, `scared`, `thinking`, `annoyed`.

Gesture:
`idle`, `explain`, `point-left`, `point-right`, `shrug`, `hands-open`, `surprised`, `thinking`, `facepalm`, `celebrate`.

Most lines should be `idle`. `explain` is useful but should not become the default raised-arm pose on every sentence. Bigger gestures are punctuation.

Gaze:
`auto`, `camera`, `left`, `right`, `up`, `down`, `away`.

Use `auto` normally. `camera` is for a deliberate fourth-wall beat only.

Listener reactions matter. Do not copy the speaker's emotion onto the listener. A skeptical look, delayed concern, suppressed amusement, or surprised reaction can carry the scene without extra dialogue.

## Background catalog

- `bedroom`: `day`, `night`, `messy-day`, `messy-night`
- `cafe`: `day`
- `classroom`: `empty`, `normal`, `exam`
- `generic-room`: `cool-day`, `warm-day`, `night`
- `hospital-room`: `day`
- `kitchen`: `day`, `night`
- `living-room`: `day`, `night`
- `office`: `day`
- `park`: `day`, `evening`
- `school-hallway`: `normal`
- `street`: `day`, `night`, `rain-night`

Tone:
`neutral`, `scary`, `happy`, `dramatic`, `cold`, `warm`.

`background_tone` describes the environment, not the speaker's emotion. A scared character in an ordinary living room should normally keep `background_tone: "neutral"`. Reserve `scary` for a genuinely ominous environment or deliberately stylized horror beat; reserve `dramatic` for a deliberately stylized environment beat. Do not make the whole room scary simply because someone is nervous, surprised, or embarrassed.

## Continuity

Preserve:
- stable cast identity;
- coherent emotional carry-over;
- consistent location during the same exchange;
- consistent day/night variant during the same exchange;
- stable left/right relationship;
- reaction logic from one line to the next.

Do not manufacture visual chaos for variety. Variety comes from story-motivated framing, reactions, location context, ambient motion, visual events, and occasional camera emphasis.

## Confidence

Score exactly:
- `concreteness`
- `variety`
- `visual_momentum`
- `speaker_readability`

`variety` should be low if the episode is effectively the same two-character composition repeated line after line. `visual_momentum` should be low if the middle has no visual events or motivated framing changes. `speaker_readability` should be low if active-speaker emphasis is absent in two-character scenes.
