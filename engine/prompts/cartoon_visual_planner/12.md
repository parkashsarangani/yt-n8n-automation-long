## Non-negotiable retry target

If a validator complains about doorway/spatial continuity, do not merely change wording. Change the actual plan fields. A passing doorway/spatial plan must have all three visible beats in order:

```text
room A scene -> crossing scene -> different room B scene
```

Use these exact field patterns when the story is about walking through doors, forgetting why you entered a room, room changes, hallway crossing, or going back to remember:

```text
room A:
background_location="living-room" or "office"
background_variant="day"
framing="establishing" or "two-shot"
camera_motion="static" or "push-in"
primary_prop="charger", "keys", "document", "coffee", or the remembered object
foreground_action="Host leaves/points to/looks for the remembered object in room A"

crossing:
background_location="hallway"
background_variant="day"
framing="doorway-transition"
camera_motion="doorway-track"
primary_prop="door"
prop_state="open threshold"
prop_motion="open"
foreground_action="Host crosses through the doorway/hallway carrying the cue"
ambient_motion="doorway-cross" or "subtle-parallax"

room B:
background_location="kitchen", "office", "bedroom", or another room different from room A
background_variant="day"
framing="establishing", "reaction-closeup", "two-shot", or "prop-insert"
camera_motion="reaction-push", "prop-focus", or "push-in"
primary_prop="charger", "keys", "document", "coffee", or the remembered object
foreground_action="Host realizes the cue changed / grabs or misses the remembered object in room B"
```

A doorway plan that never uses `background_location="hallway"`, `framing="doorway-transition"`, or `camera_motion="doorway-track"` will fail. A plan that keeps `primary_prop="door"` for many scenes will fail. The door is a transition beat; the remembered object carries the setup and payoff.

## 9.5-level visual direction target

The output must feel like a custom-directed cartoon episode, not a static two-person explainer. Every long episode must have:

1. an establishing beat;
2. a clear central object;
3. at least one prop insert;
4. at least one reaction closeup;
5. at least one motivated transition or continuity change;
6. a clean payoff shot that visually differs from the opening.

Do not use the same room, same two-shot, and same prop placement for the whole video. That is a beginner render.

## Shot language

Use `framing` to express the intended shot recipe. Allowed/common values:

- `establishing` for the first scene or a new location;
- `two-shot` for conversational base;
- `speaker-closeup` for confession, reveal, doubt, or punchline;
- `listener-closeup` when the reaction is funnier than the explanation;
- `reaction-closeup` for surprise, confusion, deadpan, or visible self-catch;
- `prop-insert` when the central object matters;
- `over-shoulder` when one character instructs another;
- `doorway-transition` for crossing/room-change beats;
- `payoff-hold` for the final changed-behavior beat.

Every 13+ scene episode must use at least four framing values. A long episode with only `two-shot` and `speaker-closeup` fails.

## Camera language

Use `camera_motion` only when motivated. Allowed/common values:

- `static` for ordinary dialogue;
- `push-in` for realization, reveal, or tension;
- `reaction-push` for visible surprise/confusion;
- `prop-focus` for a central object insert;
- `pull-out` for deflation or awkwardness;
- `pan-left` / `pan-right` when characters or locations support it;
- `doorway-track` for crossing a threshold;
- `payoff-hold` for final changed behavior.

Every 13+ scene episode must use at least two camera motion values, but do not shake the frame. Motion should feel deliberate and smooth.

## Scene continuity, not random variety

Put the characters where the situation makes sense:

- late / planning / morning routine -> bedroom, kitchen, living-room, street, or office;
- phone / message / overthinking -> bedroom or living-room;
- work message / presentation / boss -> office;
- test pressure -> classroom;
- social awkwardness / date -> cafe;
- waiting outside / walking -> park or street;
- household habit -> kitchen or living-room;
- airplane seat / cabin / window story -> airplane-cabin;
- mechanism/design/stress explanation -> office or engineering-lab.

Long episodes need at least three motivated visible environments unless the whole premise truly happens in one room. Distinct means different `background_location/background_variant` pair. Camera motion, props, and overlays do not count as environments.

For doorway/spatial-memory topics, force this continuity:

```text
living-room or office -> hallway crossing -> kitchen or different room -> return/callback -> payoff
```

Rules for doorway/spatial stories:

- the door is visible only for crossing/opening/threshold scenes;
- the door must not remain visible in more than one third of the scenes;
- there must be a clear room A, crossing, and room B;
- the remembered object, not the door, should carry the payoff;
- use `background_location="hallway"`, `framing="doorway-transition"`, and `camera_motion="doorway-track"` for at least one crossing scene.

## Foreground props and physical placement

The viewer must see the central object when the episode depends on it. A `screen-change` visual event alone is not a prop. Pair it with a concrete `primary_prop`, `prop_state`, and `foreground_action`.

Known useful props include:
`phone`, `clock`, `keys`, `calendar`, `route-map`, `door`, `coffee`, `shoes`, `window`, `bill`, `letter`, `document`, `kettle`, `food`, `locker`, `vehicle`, `tool`, `appliance`, `charger`.

Props must have physical actions:

- hand-held: held, grabbed, shown, pointed at;
- table/counter: placed, slid, left behind, picked up;
- floor: shoes, vehicle, dropped object;
- wall-mounted: clock, calendar, window;
- background set-piece: door, locker, vehicle, bed, window.

Do not describe a central object as merely `visible`. The action should say how the prop is used: `Host grabs the charger`, `keys sit on the counter`, `clock ticks behind Buddy`, `Host crosses the doorway`, `document slides across the desk`.

## Creative-direction fidelity

For each scene, map `creative_direction.scenes[]` as follows:

- `foreground_prop.type` -> `primary_prop`
- `foreground_prop.state` -> `prop_state`
- `foreground_prop.motion` -> `prop_motion`
- `foreground_prop.action` -> `foreground_action`

If `creative_direction.foreground_prop.type` is `none`, use `primary_prop: "none"`, `prop_state: "none"`, `prop_motion: "none"`, and `foreground_action: "none"` unless the script explicitly needs an object for continuity.

Before finalizing, audit the non-outro scenes:

- 13+ scenes use at least three distinct visible environments;
- no environment repeats more than five consecutive scenes;
- at least four framing values appear;
- at least two camera motion values appear;
- doorway stories include ordered room A, crossing/hallway, different room B, and payoff;
