The Turning Point is an illustrated-story channel: a single narrator tells a complete short story over sequential hand-illustrated stills (no hosts, no characters on screen, no dialogue). Every story is one of four shapes the operator or this agent selects per episode:
- **moral_story** — a parable: cold open, escalation, a low point, a turn, a payoff that reframes the opening.
- **drama** — conflict from the first line, heightened stakes, ends on an explicitly stated lesson.
- **true_story** — framed as a specific real incident, restrained/matter-of-fact voice, can end on an unresolved moral question rather than a tidy lesson.
- **short_story** — literary craft, a twist or ironic ending, no moralizing.

The channel's promise across all four: **the moment everything turns.** A strong episode sets up a situation the viewer recognizes or is drawn into, then delivers a turn — a reveal, a reversal, a consequence, a lesson earned rather than stated upfront — that recontextualizes what came before.

## Editorial target

Prefer ideas with a clear escalation and a genuine low point before the turn — a story that resolves too easily has nothing to pay off. The illustrated stills should be able to carry real visual variety (settings, objects, body language, environments) even though there are no on-screen characters with faces in every style.

Good territory includes:
- ordinary people facing a consequence they earned or didn't see coming (moral_story, drama);
- a specific real-feeling incident with a clear before/after (true_story);
- a situation with a genuine twist ending, not a moral lecture (short_story);
- workplace, family, community, and stranger conflicts people recognize from their own life;
- quiet acts of decency or cruelty that compound into a clear consequence;
- reversals of fortune, mistaken assumptions that cost something, or patience that is eventually rewarded.

The strongest candidate usually has all of these:
1. **Instant recognition or curiosity.** A viewer understands the setup in one sentence.
2. **A real stake.** Something the viewer wants resolved — vindication, relief, justice, closure.
3. **A genuine low point.** Things get worse, not just complicated, before they turn.
4. **A satisfying turn.** A reveal, reversal, or consequence that recontextualizes the opening — not a tacked-on moral.
5. **Viewer connection.** The topic creates an "I've seen that", "that's exactly what happened to me", or "I need to send this to someone" moment.
6. **A genre fit.** State which of the four genres (moral_story / drama / true_story / short_story) the idea suits best, and why.
7. **Packaging potential.** The idea implies a clean title and one visually readable opening image.

## Avoid weak automation bait

Do not propose generic listicles, broad self-help, or subjects whose only appeal is being technically unusual. This channel tells stories, not fact-dumps.

Avoid ideas that depend on breaking news, celebrity gossip, current sports results, politics, or fresh footage. The pipeline should be able to publish the episode later without the premise expiring.

Avoid contested claims about real people or events unless clearly fictionalized or handled with care — true_story should feel real without claiming to be a specific verifiable news event.

## New-channel behavior

If history is empty or `insights.sample_size` is small, do not pretend there is performance evidence. Say clearly in `basis` that the ranking is editorial judgement for a new channel.

For a new channel, deliberately test a spread across the four genres rather than eight variations of one. Still rank the candidates strongest first. Favor broadly relatable situations before obscure or niche ones.

## Do not repeat covered ground

Every topic in history is spent, whether it shipped or not. A near-duplicate premise with a different setting is still a repeat. Use `novelty` to say specifically how each candidate differs from previous coverage.

## Evidence discipline

Say what the evidence actually supports. When channel insights are weak or empty, use informed editorial judgement and label it as such. Do not write "the data shows" when the channel does not yet have enough data.
