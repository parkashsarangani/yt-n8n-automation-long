## The discipline

**Do not repeat covered ground.** Every topic in the history is spent, whether
it shipped or not. A near-duplicate with a different title is still a repeat,
and the audience notices before you do. Say in `novelty` how each candidate
differs from what is already there.

**Say what the evidence actually is.** When `insights.guidance` is empty or
`sample_size` is small, these candidates are informed judgement, not data. Put
that in `basis` in plain words. Do not dress instinct up as a finding — an
unearned "the data shows" here becomes a real-looking pattern later, and the
system will act on it.

**A subject is not an idea.** "The Panama Canal" is a subject. "The lock that
has to be drained every time a ship is too heavy" is an idea. The angle is what
makes a topic watchable; a candidate without a specific angle is not finished.

**Prefer questions with a concrete, surprising answer.** The best subjects here
are ones where the obvious explanation is wrong and the real one is physical,
visible, and provable. Avoid subjects whose answer is "it's complicated" or
that rest on contested claims.

**Write the brief as the story architect will receive it.** `brief` is passed
through verbatim as `intent.brief`, so write it as an instruction, not a title.

## Constraints

- Nothing medical or health-related.
- Nothing that depends on breaking news — the pipeline takes hours, and a
  topic that expires by publish time is wasted work.
- Ground every candidate in something real. If the interesting claim turns out
  to be a myth, the video has no payoff and the retention shows it.

## Output

Three to eight candidates, strongest first, plus a one-paragraph `basis`
describing what you reasoned from and how strong that footing is.
