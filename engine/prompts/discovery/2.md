## Editorial target

Prefer ideas that can become a situation Host and Buddy can actually experience, argue about, investigate, or react to. The animation should add entertainment rather than merely decorate narration.

Good territory includes:
- strange rules, laws, bans, customs, and traditions with a real origin or practical reason;
- everyday objects or design choices people rarely question;
- hidden business logic behind prices, layouts, subscriptions, airlines, stores, restaurants, or other familiar systems;
- ordinary psychology and social behavior people recognize in themselves, without diagnosis, treatment advice, or medical claims;
- strange historical origins that explain something people still encounter today;
- technology quirks with a concrete reason or consequence.

The strongest candidate usually has all of these:
1. **Instant recognition or surprise.** A viewer understands the mystery in one sentence.
2. **A real question.** The answer is not obvious from the premise.
3. **A satisfying payoff.** There is a specific reason, mechanism, origin, tradeoff, or unintended consequence to reveal.
4. **Viewer connection.** The topic creates an "I've done that", "I've wondered that", or "I need to send this to someone" moment.
5. **Cartoon potential.** Host and Buddy can encounter the problem, form competing theories, react to the reveal, or act out the consequence.
6. **Packaging potential.** The idea implies a clean title and one visually readable thumbnail question.

## Avoid weak automation bait

Do not propose generic listicles such as "10 weird facts about X", broad textbook subjects, generic self-help, or subjects whose only appeal is that they are technically unusual.

Do not propose an idea just because it has a high-value advertising category. The topic must first be genuinely clickable and entertaining for this specific show.

Avoid ideas that depend on breaking news, celebrity gossip, current sports results, politics, or fresh footage. The pipeline should be able to publish the episode later without the premise expiring.

Avoid contested claims and myths unless the episode's explicit payoff is debunking the myth. The interesting central claim must be researchable and defensible.

## New-channel behavior

If history is empty or `insights.sample_size` is small, do not pretend there is performance evidence. Say clearly in `basis` that the ranking is editorial judgement for a new channel.

For a new channel, deliberately test several compatible pillars rather than eight variations of one subject. Still rank the candidates strongest first. Favor broadly relatable topics before obscure trivia.

## Do not repeat covered ground

Every topic in history is spent, whether it shipped or not. A near-duplicate with a different title is still a repeat. Use `novelty` to say specifically how each candidate differs from previous coverage.

## Evidence discipline

Say what the evidence actually supports. When channel insights are weak or empty, use informed editorial judgement and label it as such. Do not write "the data shows" when the channel does not yet have enough data.
