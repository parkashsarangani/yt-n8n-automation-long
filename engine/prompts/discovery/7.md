## What every candidate must be
1. It is about YOU, the viewer. Every candidate is a moment the viewer lives through, written in second person, present tense. Never a story about someone else. No named characters, no "a woman who...", no he/she protagonist, no "people who...", no "we". Others appear only in relation to you: "your friend", "your boss", "the person you're dating".
2. Universal and instantly recognisable. Most adults have lived this exact moment in the last month. Ordinary and specific beats rare and dramatic: the drama comes from what it feels like inside, not from an unusual event. Never an extreme act most viewers have never done (a goodbye letter to a friend, quitting on the spot, a public scene). Phone and texting moments are the easiest and most overused: at most a quarter of the pool may be about messages, and none may repeat the history. Look across the whole of a life: the mirror, money, the gym, your parents, the meeting where you stayed quiet, the compliment you can't accept, the plan you keep postponing, the ex you still check on, the group photo, the queue, the bill, the bed at 3 a.m.
3. Felt, not explained. The candidate names the feeling inside the moment -- the drop in the stomach, the replay at 2 a.m., the heat in your face -- and the private thing you do because of it.
4. A truth that flips it. One real psychological mechanism, named correctly, and one real philosopher or tradition whose actual idea changes what the moment means. The best candidates carry a twist: the thing you believe about yourself in that moment is backwards.
5. Dramatic stakes. The habit quietly costs the viewer something real -- sleep, self-respect, a relationship, years of their life -- and that price can escalate within a minute.
6. Truth is the one brake: no invented studies, statistics, percentages, quotes or credentials; no diagnosis or clinical conditions; no litigation, crime, revenge or institutional victory.

## Generate a real tournament
Return 20-30 candidates, strongest first. Do not produce cosmetic variations of one moment: vary the area of life (texting, work, friendship, dating, family, money, your phone, your body, strangers, your own head at night), the feeling (dread, shame, envy, guilt, doubt, longing, irritation) and the mechanism. Anything in history is spent whether it shipped or not; the same feeling in a different setting counts as a repeat.

For every candidate return:
- `brief`: the production instruction, in second person: the moment you live, what you feel and do, the mechanism and philosopher behind it, and the reversal or verdict it builds to. Never narrate someone else.
- `genre`: legacy metadata; use "short_story".
- `angle`: the audience-facing core angle.
- `target_audience`: who lives this moment (usually: almost everyone who has a phone, a job, friends).
- `curiosity_gap`: the question the viewer needs answered about themselves.
- `emotional_engine`: the feeling the Short makes the viewer feel, and how it turns.
- `opening_visual`: one concrete image of you inside the moment.
- `opening_line`: the cold-open line, at most 14 words, second person, present tense, dropping the viewer into the moment so precisely they think "that's me". Not a question, statistic, definition, "Imagine" or greeting.
- exactly three `title_concepts` -- no more, no fewer -- one each for `curiosity`, `conflict`, `reversal`: titles a person living this would type or tap.
- exactly three `thumbnail_concepts`, one each for the same families, visually different propositions.
- `scores.clickability`, `scores.story_potential`, `scores.audience_size`, and `scores.overall` from 0..1. audience_size is high only when nearly everyone has lived the moment.
- `why_it_earns_attention`.
- `novelty` against topic history.
- `evidence` only when real measured episodes support the choice.

The three packaging families must differ psychologically:
- curiosity = an unanswered question about why YOU do this;
- conflict = the painful gap between what you do and what you want;
- reversal = the thing you believe about yourself that is backwards.

## Ranking discipline
Rank by how instantly and painfully a stranger recognises themselves, then by how hard the reversal lands. Score honestly; do not inflate everything above 0.8. A candidate that is clever but not recognisable, or recognisable but with nothing surprising behind it, ranks low. If measured performance is thin or absent, say that plainly in `basis`.

## No generic automation bait
No listicles, broad self-help, fact dumps, "be kind" morals, or abstract topics ("overthinking", "anxiety") without the concrete moment. Every candidate starts from one precise moment.

## Hard length limits
Every candidate is validated against these limits and a candidate that breaks one is discarded, so stay well inside them. Count characters, not words.
- `brief`: at most 400 characters (about 60 words). Two or three dense sentences.
- `angle`: at most 300. `why_it_earns_attention`: at most 400. `novelty`: at most 300. `opening_visual`: at most 320.
- `target_audience`, `curiosity_gap`, `opening_line`: at most 220 each. `emotional_engine`: at most 180.
- Each title at most 100; each thumbnail concept at most 260. `evidence`: at most 500. `basis`: at most 900.

Every field listed above is required on every candidate, including `emotional_engine`. A candidate missing any of them is discarded.
