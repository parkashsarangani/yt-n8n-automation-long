## Generate a real tournament

Return 20-30 candidates, strongest first. Do not produce eight cosmetic variations of one premise. The pool should be broad enough that the system can discard weak ideas instead of forcing every idea into production.

For the learning-stage channel, bias the pool toward a recognizable repeatable emotional promise unless measured evidence says otherwise:

injustice / underestimation -> escalation -> lowest moment -> competence, truth, or sacrifice revealed -> reversal / vindication / consequence.

Settings may vary widely: workplace, family, school, neighborhood, customer/service, friendship, small business, community, strangers. The emotional promise may repeat; the concrete premise must not.

Do not treat moral_story, drama, true_story, and short_story as equally weighted random buckets. Choose the genre that makes each candidate strongest. Early on, coherent audience promise matters more than genre diversity for its own sake.

## Every candidate is already packageable

For every candidate return:

- `brief`: production-ready story instruction with concrete setup, escalation, low point and intended turn/payoff.
- `genre`.
- `angle`: audience-facing core angle.
- `target_audience`: the kind of viewer who immediately recognizes or cares about this situation.
- `curiosity_gap`: the unresolved question the click creates.
- `emotional_engine`: the emotional movement that makes the episode worth finishing.
- `opening_visual`: one concrete image that can carry the first 1-2 seconds by itself.
- `opening_line`: the strongest cold-open line. It should be the strongest narrative beat in the episode, not neutral setup.
- exactly three `title_concepts`, one each for `curiosity`, `conflict`, `reversal`.
- exactly three `thumbnail_concepts`, one each for the same families. They must be visually different propositions, not the same composition with different text.
- `scores.clickability`, `scores.story_potential`, `scores.audience_size`, and `scores.overall` from 0..1.
- `why_it_earns_attention`.
- `novelty` against topic history.
- `evidence` only when real measured episodes support the choice.

The three packaging families must differ psychologically:
- curiosity = unanswered question / missing information;
- conflict = unfairness, tension, high-stakes choice, social friction;
- reversal = consequence, vindication, surprising outcome.

## Ranking discipline

Rank by expected growth opportunity, not intellectual interest. A good candidate has:
1. a title/thumbnail proposition a stranger can understand immediately;
2. a visually strong opening moment;
3. a genuine question that survives beyond the first line;
4. escalating stakes and a real low point;
5. a turn/payoff worth waiting for;
6. broad recognizability or intense relevance to a definable audience;
7. enough concrete visual situations to sustain sequential illustrated shots;
8. no dependence on breaking news, celebrity gossip, current politics, sports results, or expiring footage.

Score honestly. Do not inflate everything above 0.8. A candidate with weak packaging or a story that collapses after the hook should be ranked low even if the subject is interesting.

## Evidence discipline

If measured performance is thin or absent, say that plainly in `basis` and use editorial judgement. If real retention/CTR patterns exist, use them concretely: prefer structures that retained viewers, avoid repeated early-drop patterns, and cite the observed pattern in `evidence` without pretending correlation is certainty.

## Do not repeat spent ground

Anything in history is spent whether it shipped or not. A near-identical emotional beat in a different superficial setting counts as a repeat if the audience proposition is the same.

## No generic automation bait

No listicles, broad self-help, generic "life lesson" premises, fact dumps, or stories whose turn is merely "be kind". The Turning Point needs a particular situation, a concrete escalation, and a reversal/consequence that changes how the opening is understood.

## Channel fit and earned progression
Everyday psychology is this channel's default. intent.niche=everyday-psychology overrides legacy genre labels and off-niche package seeds. Reframe unsuitable seeds around one recognisable thinking trap in an ordinary moment: misreading tone, overthinking a silence, sunk cost, anchoring, the spotlight effect, procrastination, comparison, or refusing to change your mind. No litigation, crime, revenge, institutional victory or clinical conditions as the main promise. In this niche apply series editorial and scene-role rules even without intent.series, but never invent numbered curriculum, assigned exercises or published sequels. Give a self-contained practical exercise when none is supplied.
Show the automatic first reaction, a plausible complication, and a reconsidered reaction with a proportionate outcome. No convenient confession, surprise evidence, perfect authority or sudden cooperation to solve the problem. Give useful progress early while preserving a later choice worth following.
Deliver the central payoff near the end, then a concise takeaway and one optional discussion invitation. No extended aftermath, repeated recap or second closing question.

Every candidate brief must name one thinking trap or mental habit and an ordinary setting. Candidate genre is legacy metadata. Exclude revenge and legal-victory premises even when old history contains them; propose standalone lessons with a complication and feasible exercise.

## Hard length limits

Every candidate is validated against these limits and a candidate that breaks one is discarded, so stay well inside them. Count characters, not words.

- `brief`: at most 400 characters (about 60 words). Two or three dense sentences: setting, skill, complication, turn. Do not narrate the whole episode.
- `angle`: at most 300. `why_it_earns_attention`: at most 400. `novelty`: at most 300. `opening_visual`: at most 320.
- `target_audience`, `curiosity_gap`, `opening_line`: at most 220 each. `emotional_engine`: at most 180.
- Each title at most 100; each thumbnail concept at most 260. `evidence`: at most 500. `basis`: at most 900.

Every field listed above is required on every candidate, including `emotional_engine`. A candidate missing any of them is discarded.
