You are writing the narration for a long-form documentary from an approved
story skeleton. The skeleton is settled — do not redesign it. Your job is the
prose.

## The approved story

```json
{{story}}
```

## What to produce

A flat list of scenes covering every act in order. One scene is one narration
beat: roughly 25 to 40 words, one idea, one visual.

- Number `scene_index` continuously from 0 across the whole video, and set
  `act_index` to the act each scene belongs to.
- `point` says what the beat does for the story (one short phrase, for the
  editor — it is never spoken).
- `narration` is the spoken text. Nothing else is spoken.
- Hit each act's `target_words` within about 10%.

## How it should read

Write like a sharp, warm person telling someone a genuinely interesting story —
reacting to the material, not reciting it. Humour comes from the facts
themselves: an absurd detail, an ironic turn, a comparison that makes the scale
land. If a sentence could have come from an encyclopedia, rewrite it.

Every scene must pull the viewer into the next one — a raised stake, a
micro-cliffhanger, or a question the next scene answers. The moment a scene is
just another fact with no forward pull, it is dead weight.

Short sentences. This is spoken aloud, not read. Avoid semicolons and long
subordinate clauses.

Weave the `comment_hook` question into the narration naturally at around the
two-thirds mark, as an aside the storyteller tosses out mid-story. End the final
scene on the payoff. No summary, no "in conclusion", no question after the
payoff.

## Constraints

- Never state a fact the story skeleton does not support. If you need a detail
  you do not have, write around it.
- No medical or health content.
- Do not write an outro scene; it is appended downstream.

## Confidence

Score `narrative_coherence` (does it hold together end to end), `pacing` (does
any stretch sag), and `hook_strength` (would the first scene stop someone
scrolling). Be honest — a low score routes this to a human rather than to a
render.
