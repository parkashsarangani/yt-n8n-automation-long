HOW TO WRITE IT:
1. HOOK (first 2-3 seconds, 12-30 words). The FIRST sentence is at most 12 words and stops the scroll: a contradiction, a bold claim, or a painfully familiar "you" moment ("You're not tired. You're bored." / "You said yes again. You didn't want to."). By the end of the hook the viewer must know what they will get AND have a reason to stay to the end (an open loop: "the third one is the one nobody does"). No greeting, no channel name, no "in this video", no "today we'll talk about".
2. BEATS. After the hook, write 6-9 beats, then the closing line. Each beat is ONE paragraph of 15-30 words (5-10 seconds) and ONE concrete moment that could be drawn: a person, a place, an action ("Your coworker drops a stack of files on your desk at 5:58."). Never a beat of pure abstraction.
3. RE-HOOK every 3 beats or so: one short line that reopens curiosity ("But that's not the part that hurts." / "Here's where it flips." / "And this is the trap."). Vary them; never use the same one twice.
4. ESCALATE. Each beat raises the stakes or gets more useful; the strongest, most surprising payoff is the LAST body beat, never the first.
5. PAYOFFS are specific enough to picture and to try today ("put the phone in the other room before you open the laptop"). Where a real psychological effect or finding, or a real philosophical idea or thinker (Stoicism, Epictetus, Camus, the dichotomy of control...), explains a point, name it correctly in one plain sentence.
6. CLOSING LINE (its own paragraph, max 20 words): a question the viewer can answer in a word or two that LOOPS back to the hook, so watching again feels natural. Do NOT ask viewers to like, subscribe or follow -- a follow line is added automatically.
7. RHYTHM: short spoken sentences, second person, present tense where possible. No filler words, no throat-clearing, no summaries of what was just said.

LENGTH: hook + all beats + closing line together 160-205 words (about 60-72 seconds at the narrator's pace).

CLICKBAIT IS WELCOME; INVENTED FACTS ARE NOT. The title and hook may be bold, curiosity-driven and emotional. But every factual claim must be true:
- NO percentages and NO invented statistics ("98% of people", "80% faster"). If you cite research, describe it honestly and without numbers you cannot stand behind.
- Use psychology terms and philosophical ideas correctly, and never put invented words in a real philosopher's mouth -- paraphrase unless you are certain of a quote. If you are not sure an effect or idea is real and correctly described, leave the name out and just give the advice. The editor will check every named effect and idea.
- No medical or therapy claims, no diagnosis. Do not call ordinary advice "dark psychology" or "manipulation".

TITLE: one YouTube title, 30-80 characters, curiosity-driven, promising a clear payoff, and not a copy of the hook's first sentence. Also give two alternative titles in different styles.
