## Required explanation fields

- `scene_role`: `character-hook`, `diagram-build`, `process-flow`, `object-state-change`, `comparison`, `kinetic-emphasis`, `character-reaction`, or `recap`
- `visual_operation`: `stack`, `timeline`, `counter`, `compress`, `group`, `sort`, `scale-compare`, or `payoff`
- `visual_primitive`: select the subject or relationship primitive from the grammar below
- `visual_state`: exactly one of `hypothesis`, `contradiction`, `mechanism`, `qualification`, `payoff`. These are the five states the renderer can draw, and they are NOT the script's beat names. Map the beat you are illustrating onto one of them:
  - hook, intuitive answer, or any guess still being entertained -> `hypothesis` (drawn provisionally, dashed)
  - objection, the gap, the guess failing -> `contradiction` (the geometry visibly breaks)
  - visual model, correction, the accurate explanation -> `mechanism` (solid, asserted construction)
  - implication, limit, exception, "only when" -> `qualification`
  - recap, the resolved closing model -> `payoff`
  Never put a beat name such as `correction`, `implication` or `takeaway` in this field. An off-vocabulary value is snapped to a valid one automatically, so the scene still renders -- in the wrong state, with no error to tell you.
- `composition_mode`: `bookend`, `full-model`, or `reaction`
- `explanation_title`: a short orienting label, not a sentence
- `model_elements`: 0-5 concrete labels the viewer can scan
- `numeric_value`: whenever a scene uses `visual_primitive: "quantity"` OR `visual_operation: "counter"`, this field is mandatory and must be a bare JSON number (`73`, not `"73"`, not `null`, not `"about 73"`). Omit the field entirely for every other scene. A single scene that breaks this rule rejects the whole plan, so check each quantity/counter scene before returning.
- `state_before` and `state_after`: required for state changes/comparisons, otherwise empty strings
- `key_text`: the one phrase worth emphasizing, otherwise empty
- `character_cut_in`: `none`, `speaker`, `listener`, or `both`
- `sound_cue`: `none`, `soft-hit`, `tick`, `whoosh`, `pop`, or `resolve`

Keep model labels under five words. Do not copy whole narration lines into graphics. A viewer should understand what changed with the sound muted.

## Visual operation choice

Every scene must choose the concrete motion the viewer will watch. A semantic role alone is not a visual plan.

- `stack`: labeled objects accumulate into a visible stack.
- `timeline`: milestones populate while a playhead keeps advancing.
- `counter`: a quantity changes while matching items activate.
- `compress`: separate items converge and collapse into a smaller representation.
- `group`: scattered objects move into meaningful clusters.
- `sort`: unordered items visibly reorder.
- `scale-compare`: two sides change size relative to one another.
- `payoff`: the opening model resolves into one decisive final state.

Primitive-operation compatibility is enforced by the compiler:
- `stack`: `particles`, `objects`, `hierarchy`, `nested-context`, `quantity`, or `shells`
- `timeline`: `timeline`, `cause-chain`, `path`, `map`, `rays`, `wave`, `spectrum`, `cycle`, or `particles`
- `counter`: `quantity`, `particles`, or `objects`
- `compress`: `particles`, `objects`, `many-to-one`, `physical-transformation`, `before-after`, or `shells`
- `group`: `network`, `one-to-many`, `many-to-one`, `facets-around-center`, `overlapping-sets`, `nested-context`, `particles`, or `objects`
- `sort`: `objects`, `hierarchy`, `quantity`, or `timeline`
- `scale-compare`: `before-after`, `physical-transformation`, `spectrum`, `quantity`, `objects`, or `overlapping-sets`
- `payoff`: any declared primitive, only for the final resolved callback

The primitive must depict the subject or the exact relationship being explained:

Subject primitives:
- `particles`: many-instance systems such as stars, cells, people, grains, or votes.
- `rays`: sightlines, beams, influence, direction, or coverage.
- `wave`: oscillation, sound, frequency, or repeating change.
- `horizon`: reach limits, boundaries, visibility, thresholds, or finite access.
- `spectrum`: wavelength, color, temperature bands, or continuous ranges.
- `path`: travel, arrival, signal movement, or a journey.
- `shells`: depth, layers, generations, or expanding regions.
- `objects`: only when no subject or relationship primitive fits.

Relationship primitives:
- `network`: many entities with meaningful cross-connections.
- `hierarchy`: ranked, parent-child, taxonomic, or branching structure.
- `one-to-many`: one source produces or appears as several outcomes.
- `many-to-one`: several inputs converge on one result.
- `facets-around-center`: several viewpoints, attributes, or forms surround one shared center.
- `overlapping-sets`: categories overlap without becoming identical.
- `nested-context`: an item changes meaning inside wider layers or contexts.
- `cycle`: a repeating causal loop.
- `cause-chain`: one event produces the next in a causal sequence.
- `before-after`: explicit state comparison.
- `map`: movement or spatial relationship across locations.
- `timeline`: ordered development through time.
- `quantity`: accumulation, depletion, or count.
- `physical-transformation`: one concrete state visibly becomes another.

Use the relationship primitive whenever the claim is relational. Do not force a wave, ray, stack, or generic card onto a social, historical, philosophical, or religious relationship.

Never use labels such as "star icon", "microwave icon", "arrow", "marker", "card", or "ribbon" as substitutes for drawing the thing. Use concrete content labels such as "near stars", "visible red", or "observable edge".

The operation must progress throughout the spoken turn, not finish in the first second. Choose the operation that proves the line's claim. Use at least four operation types in episodes with 8+ scenes, do not repeat one operation more than twice consecutively, and use `payoff` for the final `recap`. Internal role and operation names are production metadata and must never be written into titles, labels, or key text.

## Visual certainty and composition

The renderer has a visual grammar for epistemic state:

- `hypothesis`: dashed, provisional and visibly unstable. Use for the plausible wrong answer.
- `contradiction`: the wrong model collides with evidence or is visibly crossed/broken.
- `mechanism`: solid connections and controlled motion reveal how the system works.
- `qualification`: boundaries, exceptions, overlap or contextual variation enter without erasing the mechanism.
- `payoff`: the opening model returns in its simplest resolved form.

Composition:
- `bookend`: mandatory for scene 0 and final recap; both characters visible.
- `full-model`: default for explanation; the model owns at least 65% of the usable frame.
- `reaction`: a deliberate middle character return for disagreement, humor, status shift, or realization.

A scene must play as a visual sentence: setup, anticipation, transformation, consequence, then a short final hold. The primitive defines what exists; `visual_operation` defines what happens to it. Never select an operation that merely renames the primitive. Wrong and correct models must never use identical styling. Use no more than three visible labels unless a fourth is necessary for a causal sequence.

## Role choice

- opening tension or human question -> `character-hook`
- assembling a mental model -> `diagram-build`
- causal sequence -> `process-flow`
- one thing visibly transforms -> `object-state-change`
- explicit contrast -> `comparison`
- twist or memorable phrase -> `kinetic-emphasis`
- joke, disbelief, emotional consequence -> `character-reaction`
- final mechanism and behavior -> `recap`

Use at least three explanation roles in episodes with 8+ scenes. Never repeat the same role more than four consecutive times.

## Character restraint

Use `both` primarily for the hook. Use `listener` for reaction beats. Prefer `none` while teaching the mechanism. The speaker remains audible even when off-screen.

## Character performance fields

For `bookend` and `reaction`, provide listener actor ID plus speaker/listener emotion, gesture, and gaze grounded in the spoken turn. Omit those performance fields from `full-model` scenes because no character is rendered. Do not emit or optimize legacy background, framing, camera, ambient-motion, visual-event, emphasis, or cutaway fields; the compiler supplies neutral compatibility defaults internally.
