## What you produce

### primary_keyword

The one phrase this listing is aimed at. Pick it from how a person would
actually type the question into search — not from how the story words it. A
narration saying "the Andes drew this border long before any treaty did"
targets `why is chile so long`, not `andean geological border formation`.

Choose something a real person searches for and this video genuinely answers.

### title — at most 100 characters, aim for under 60

100 is YouTube's hard limit. About 60 characters survive before truncation on
mobile and in sidebars, so treat 60 as the real budget and 100 as the cliff.

- Put the primary keyword near the front. Trailing keywords get truncated away.
- The story's title is a *narrative* title. It may be a bad listing title.
  Rewriting it is your job; keeping it is a choice you should be able to defend.
- Promise exactly what the narration delivers. A title the video does not pay
  off costs more in watch time than it gains in clicks, and the algorithm reads
  the second signal more strongly than the first.
- No ALL CAPS, no manufactured outrage, no "you won't believe".

### description — the first 150 characters are the only ones most people see

Structure it in three parts:

1. **Opening (~150 chars).** A real sentence containing the primary keyword,
   written for a human who is deciding whether to click. This is what shows in
   search results. Do not waste it on "In this video, we will..."
2. **Body.** Two or three short paragraphs on what the video actually covers,
   using the words a searcher would use. Pull real specifics out of the
   narration — a concrete number or name in the description is worth more than
   another keyword.
3. **Close.** One line inviting a comment, tied to the story's own open
   question if it has one.

Do not put a chapter list in — chapters need real timings, which do not exist
yet at this point in the pipeline, and inventing them would be worse than
having none.

### tags — 5 to 15 of them

YouTube's real constraint is **500 characters in total across every tag**, not
a tag count, so keep them short and stop when they stop adding meaning. Six
strong tags beat fifteen padded ones.

Cover three kinds: the primary keyword, two or three close variants of how
people phrase it, and two or three broader topic terms. Tags carry far less
weight than they used to; they are a tie-breaker, not a strategy. Do not stuff
them, and do not include tags the video does not address.

### rationale

Two or three sentences: who you think searches for this, and why this title
earns the click over whatever currently ranks. Recorded so an underperforming
listing can be diagnosed rather than re-guessed.

### alternative_titles — optional, up to 4

Genuinely different angles, strongest first — not rewordings. These become the
variants for a title A/B test later.
