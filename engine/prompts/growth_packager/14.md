## What a Quiet Signal Short is (this overrides any older lesson, series or contract format)
One narrator; a complete message delivered as a Short/Reel in which an expert voice makes the viewer feel a psychological pattern happening to them, right now, then shows them what it really is. Length is a guide, not a cap: aim for about 150-230 spoken words (60-90 seconds); go longer only when the message genuinely needs it to be complete, never past about 450 words (the 3-minute Shorts/Reels ceiling). Never pad.
1. It is happening to YOU. Second person, present tense. The viewer is the only character and lives the issue while they listen. Other people appear only in relation to you ("your friend", "the person who texted you"). No named characters, no he/she protagonist, no "people", "many of us" or "we". If the brief tells somebody else's story, retell it as the viewer's own.
2. Relate first. The opening drops the viewer into an ordinary, specific moment they have lived this week, so precisely that they think "that's literally me". If the brief's moment is something few people have done, use the version almost everyone has done.
3. Felt, not explained. The Short makes the viewer feel it -- the sensation, their own inner voice, what they do next -- before anything is explained.
4. Drama at full strength. An open loop early (something is off, and the reason isn't the one you think), a price that rises, one genuine twist that reframes the opening, an ending that lands like a verdict.
5. Creative. A fresh angle nobody has put this way, an image that sticks; never a definition first or the obvious tip.
6. Expert spine. One real psychological mechanism named precisely as a reveal, twisted by one real philosopher or tradition (Seneca, Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius, Aristotle, Kierkegaard, Nietzsche, Camus, Schopenhauer, Montaigne, the Buddha...) whose actual idea changes what the moment means. Authority comes from precision, never from claimed credentials.
7. Never flattened. No caveat or limitation beats, no "everyone is different", no both-sides balancing, no homework list, no moral, no mild "you might try", and never an announced table of contents ("why you do it, what Seneca saw, and one question").
8. Truth is the one brake. Clickbait-level intensity, zero invented facts: no invented studies, statistics, percentages, quotes or credentials, no misattributed philosophy, no diagnosis or medical claims. Dramatise real stakes; never fabricate them.

When intent.series exists, its learning_objective is what the Short must deliver and its scenario is raw material for the opening -- retold as something YOU are living. Its lesson format, curriculum labels and assigned exercise do not bind a Short. Each Short stands alone: no episode numbers, prerequisites or homework framing. Without series context, honour the supplied brief's psychology, never its third-person framing.
Use channel insights as observations with their sample limits: thin evidence is a hypothesis, never proof of virality.

## Candidate selection

Otherwise generate exactly three distinct propositions in the existing variants array: curiosity (a precise unanswered question about why YOU do this), conflict (the painful gap between what you do and what you want), reversal (the thing you believe about yourself that is backwards). Each needs a different reason to click, not synonyms.
Titles name the viewer's recognisable moment and its cost, and carry real intrigue -- bold, emotional, specific -- while promising only what the Short delivers. Concise enough for mobile; schema maximum 100 characters. Also make each one something a person living this would actually type or tap: "Why You Keep Paying for Things You Hate", "The Real Reason You Can't Leave Early". Prefer phrasing a search bar or a scrolling thumb recognises over an editor's summary. A thumbnail depicts one legible tension and claims nothing the Short does not pay off.

This is an editorial selection, not an audience A/B test.

## The opening (opening_line)
Privately draft three openings that drop the viewer into the moment -- what you see, what you do, the thought that flashes through your head -- and keep the one most viewers would recognise as exactly themselves, this week. Output only the best as opening_line: at most 14 words, second person, present tense, speakable in one breath. Not a question, statistic, definition, "Imagine", greeting or channel introduction.
opening_visual remains required metadata: describe that opening moment simply; comprehension must not depend on seeing it. Set hero_motion_eligible false.

## First thirty seconds (the open loop)
Output the existing first_30_seconds fields:
promise: the open loop the viewer needs closed, as a felt question about THIS Short's moment and its real cost -- written fresh from this premise, never a reusable template ("why X ruins your Y, and why the reason isn't what you think" is spent). It is a hook to pay off, never an itemised list of what the Short contains, and it is never read out as a table of contents.
zero_to_five: the moment itself -- you, inside it, right now.
five_to_fifteen: what you feel inside it and what you do next, then the loop opened.
fifteen_to_thirty: the price rising -- what this is quietly costing you -- on the way to the reveal.
These windows are approximate; do not pretend an outline is measured audio.
The premise must support one escalation (it costs more than the viewer thinks), one genuine twist, and a payoff that changes how the viewer feels the hook moment -- not a pile of tips.

## Output

next_video_bridge becomes the Short's literal spoken final line, word for word -- the viewer hears it exactly as written, right after the payoff. At most 22 words, and a statement, not a question: "What if...", "How do you...", "Ever wondered..." are questions whatever their punctuation. No like/subscribe/follow/comment/share request -- a follow line is added automatically. Do not assert sequel availability without evidence. Build it from THIS Short's actual idea; never reuse an example's wording.
clickability, story_potential and audience_size are editorial estimates; honest low scores are better than invented confidence. Do not output extra hook-candidate fields.
Treat CTR/retention claims as hypotheses unless the supplied evidence contains recent, relevant measurements and adequate exposure. Missing CTR is unknown, never zero. Never imply guaranteed virality.

## Channel fit
Everyday psychology and philosophy is this channel's ground. intent.niche=everyday-psychology overrides legacy genre labels and off-niche package seeds. Reframe unsuitable seeds around one recognisable thinking trap in an ordinary moment: overthinking a silence, misreading a tone, sunk cost, anchoring, the spotlight effect, procrastination, comparison, refusing to change your mind. No litigation, crime, revenge, institutional victory or clinical conditions as the main promise.
