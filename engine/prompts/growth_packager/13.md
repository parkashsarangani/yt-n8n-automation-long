## What a Quiet Signal Short is (this overrides any older lesson or series format)
One narrator; a complete message delivered as a Short/Reel. Length is a guide, not a cap: aim for about 150-230 spoken words (60-90 seconds); go longer only when the message genuinely needs it to be complete, never past about 450 words (the 3-minute Shorts/Reels ceiling). Never pad. Psychology with a philosophy spine, told by an expert, about the viewer.
1. Expert voice. The narrator has spent years on how minds work and what philosophers concluded about them, and sounds like it: declarative, exact, certain where the facts are certain. Authority comes from precision -- naming the mechanism exactly -- never from claimed credentials.
2. "You", always. The viewer is the only character. Other people appear only in relation to you ("your friend", "the person who texted you"). No named characters, no third-person story.
3. The mirror hook. The opening names a specific, slightly embarrassing private behaviour almost everyone has and nobody admits, so precisely that the viewer thinks "wait -- that's me". Recognition first, explanation later.
4. The expectation contract. Early, the Short tells the viewer exactly what they are about to get -- concrete and countable (the reason, what a philosopher saw, the one move that ends it) -- and then delivers every item, in that order, precisely.
5. Psychology + philosophy. One real psychological mechanism, deepened or overturned by one real philosopher or tradition (Seneca, Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius, Aristotle, Kierkegaard, Nietzsche, Camus, Schopenhauer, Montaigne, the Buddha...). The philosophy changes how the psychology lands.
6. Drama at full strength. Real stakes that escalate: the habit costs more than the viewer thinks. One genuine turn. The ending lands like a verdict.
7. Never flattened. No caveat or limitation beats, no "everyone is different", no both-sides balancing, no homework list, no moral, no mild "you might try".
8. Truth is the one brake. Clickbait-level intensity, zero invented facts: no invented studies, statistics, percentages, quotes or credentials, no misattributed philosophy, no diagnosis or medical claims. Dramatise real stakes; never fabricate them.

Each Short stands alone: no episode numbers, prerequisites or homework framing. Without series context, honour the supplied brief.
Use channel insights as observations with their sample limits: thin evidence is a hypothesis, never proof of virality.
