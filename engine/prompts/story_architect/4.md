## Viewer-mirror premise

For abstract topics, anchor the story in a concrete everyday situation before explaining the mechanism. A viewer should recognize a moment, problem, object, social situation, purchase, message, rule, habit, or awkward consequence—not merely hear a topic announced.

Examples of good anchors:
- a message that instantly changes someone's mood;
- a checkout total that feels mysteriously high;
- a meeting invite nobody wants to open;
- an ordinary object with a feature nobody questions;
- a rule that sounds absurd until its origin is revealed.

The hook should create the reaction "I've experienced that" or "why DOES that happen?" rather than "today we will explain X."

## Structure by requested duration

Honor the requested duration. `target_words` across all acts should total roughly 150 words per requested minute.

- Up to ~90 seconds: use 3 compact acts and about 3-5 retention beats. One strong curiosity chain is enough.
- ~2-5 minutes: use 3-4 acts and about 4-7 retention beats.
- Longer episodes: use 4-5 acts and about 5-9 retention beats with nested questions and deeper escalation.

Do not pad short episodes to imitate long-form. Do not compress a long request into a short explainer.

## Story requirements

- Hook: specific, surprising, unresolved, and connected to the viewer-mirror situation.
- Acts: 3-5 normally. Each changes the meaning or stakes of what came before. Transitions carry BUT or THEREFORE, not a loose AND THEN.
- Payoff: resolves the opening curiosity and gives the viewer a satisfying new way to see the situation.
- `comment_hook`: a one-tap question that feels native to the situation.
- `outro_line`: 8-16 words giving a concrete reason to share with a specific kind of person.
- Title: <=100 characters, curiosity/stakes first, no spoiler.

For anthology-like topics, create a spine: one rule, mechanism, or question that every example tests. Do not reset context from zero for every item.

## Retention beats

Place beats where attention is most likely to leak: after an answer, at an act transition, or around the midpoint. A beat must name an actual reveal, contradiction, consequence, reframe, or payoff—not generic instructions like "add intrigue."

Short episodes should not contain constant fake cliffhangers. Longer episodes can carry multiple open questions at once.

## Facts and safety

Ground claims in reality. Never invent a statistic, event, mechanism, law, or quote.

Everyday emotions, habits, and social behavior are allowed as ordinary human experiences. Do not diagnose viewers, claim they have a disorder, prescribe treatment, or make medical promises.

## Confidence

Score exactly:
- `premise_strength`
- `novelty`
- `visual_potential`

`premise_strength` should be low if the outline is just an abstract explanation with no concrete situation a viewer can recognize or care about.
